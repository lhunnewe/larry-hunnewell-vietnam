# Unresolved questions

Working notes for the archive's open research questions. Each question gets its own file as
research begins. The public summary lives on the website's Research page; conclusions only move
into `data/` when the evidence supports a classification change.

Open questions:

1. **The KIA report** — locate the Stars and Stripes item (~August 1965, Pacific edition), any
   correction, casualty documentation, and the explanation for the error. *DD-214 (Aug 2026):
   his last duty assignment was in Japan under USARJ, so the remembered in-Japan sighting is
   documentary-plausible; search window widened to ~Aug–Nov 1965 (separated 30 Nov 65). Call
   of 2026-08-12: he arrived in Japan ~early September as a medevac patient, so the likely
   window is Sept–Nov 1965 and he may have seen the paper in the hospital ward — ask.*
2. **The unit** — verify or correct "118th AOD — Aviation Operations Detachment"; explain the
   remembered 1st Cavalry association (note: 1st Cavalry Division arrived in-country ~Sep 1965).
   *The DD-214 does not settle this — item 11 names only the last duty assignment (Japan). Still
   waiting on the NPRC file, which the DD-214's dates and numbers now let us request precisely.*
   *2026-08-13 sweep (research/units/callsigns-and-units.md): no unit designated "118th AOD"
   existed; the 118th Aviation Company (Airmobile Light) — "Thunderbirds"/"Bandits," Bien Hoa,
   III Corps, documented standby-alert missions in 1965 — is the leading hypothesis ("AML"
   heard as "AOD"). Its veteran site is http://www.118ahc.us/ (corrected from an earlier
   dead .org address) with a dedicated 118th Aviation Company (AML) 1963–65 history page.*

   Núi Bà Đen — further supporting Tân Sơn Nhứt.*
4. **Sgt. Lowry / Lowery** — Special Forces sergeant on Núi Bà Đen, spelling and identity.
   *2026-08-13: now also the only named witness to the "450 civilians" day (#10) — he was
   the lone SF man at the camp and delivered the accusation. Identifying him could unlock
   the whole incident. Web sweep found no trace (expected for a surviving NCO); avenues are
   Special Forces Association rolls and NARA 5th SFG / B-32 rosters. Context established:
   the official Army history documents the Núi Bà Đen CIDG camp at half A-team strength —
   1 officer, 5 enlisted — in October 1964, so "the only one there from Special Forces that
   day" is entirely consistent with the documented garrison.*

7. **The Japan medical command** — **largely answered by Larry himself (call, 2026-08-12):**

   hepatitis — he described pure-white stool and Coca-Cola-dark urine — plus malaria and
   infections), arriving ~early September 1965. The Tokyo photo series belongs to this
   hospital period, with a fellow-patient friend. *Research (2026-08-12,
   research/places/japan-hospital-1965.md): the hospital was almost certainly the U.S. Army
   Medical Center, Japan at Sagami-Ōno / Camp Zama — the only Army hospital in Japan taking
   Vietnam evacuees before Dec 1965, with 462 malaria admissions Aug–Dec 1965.* Still open:
   NPRC clinical-records confirmation (filed by hospital name), admission/discharge dates,

   research/records-request/dd214-findings.md. *2026-08-13: Larry named the hospital
   himself, **unprompted** — told only that research had a high-confidence candidate, not
   which — writing "I was at Camp Zama in Japan in Sugami Uro" [Sagami-Ōno]. The
   identification is now independently confirmed by testimony; NPRC clinical records remain
