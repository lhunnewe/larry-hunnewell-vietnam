# The 145th Aviation Battalion was at Tan Son Nhut

**Found 2026-09-03**, in the first hour of the Stars and Stripes sweep (ledger #2, issues #10,
#11, #57). Source: Pacific Stars and Stripes 1964–65, via the NewspaperArchive Stars and
Stripes portal. **Research file only — nothing here goes into a rendered field yet. See
"Handling" at the bottom.**

## The finding

A Pacific Stars and Stripes item of **5 November 1965**, about the "Fangs" of the **6th Aviation
Platoon**, quotes them on their own change of assignment: they were *"assigned to the 145th
Aviation Bn at Tan Son Nhut."*

**The 145th Aviation Battalion was headquartered at Tan Son Nhut.** And the 118th Aviation
Company belonged to the 145th — which the same sweep confirms repeatedly, and which the Coffelt
casualty records confirmed independently on 2026-09-02.

## Why this matters more than anything else found so far

The archive's central structural problem has been a contradiction it could not resolve:

- Larry remembers **"118th AOD"** and places his **company headquarters at "Thom Son Nuht"**
  (Tan Son Nhut), where he worked twelve-hour shifts alone in a radio van as a **flight
  controller** doing **flight following** for what he calls "Saigon center."
- But the **118th Aviation Company was at Bien Hoa**, not Tan Son Nhut. This sweep confirms that
  twice over — awards presented to the company at Bien Hoa Airfield in January 1964, and
  "the 118th Aviation Co's headquarters at Bien Hoa" in November 1965.

That mismatch has sat in ledger #2 since 2026-08-13 and was the main reason to doubt the 118th.

**The resolution is that both are true, one level apart.** The company was at Bien Hoa; its
**battalion was at Tan Son Nhut**. A battalion headquarters is precisely where an operations
section — flight following, a radio van, shift-working flight controllers talking to subordinate
companies — would sit. Larry's "Saigon center" and his "only annex of it" on the mountain fit a
battalion-level net, not a company flight line.

**Working hypothesis, not a conclusion: Larry was at 145th Aviation Battalion HQ at Tan Son
Nhut**, and "118th AOD" is his memory of the battalion's best-known company plus an operations
detachment. It also fits his 2026-08-23 statement that the van stood "in the property of 18th
aviation" — a large aviation installation, not his own company's.

**What would confirm it:** a morning report or unit roster placing him in the 145th Avn Bn HQ
or one of its detachments (#27 NPRC/RG 472), or Larry naming the battalion himself unprompted.

## The 145th Aviation Battalion, assembled from period Stars and Stripes

**Location:** Tan Son Nhut (5 Nov 1965).

**Higher command, 1964:** *U.S. Army Support Command, Vietnam* — given plainly in an item of
24 April 1964 stating the 118th Aviation Co was part of it. A February 1964 item renders it
"U.S. Army Support Group, Vietnam." **This is the correct 1964 higher command, and it settles a
caveat raised on 2026-09-02:** Coffelt's casualty records list the major command as "1st Avn
Bde," which did not exist until March 1966. Coffelt is normalised; these are period.

**Commanding officers, in sequence — a full 1964–65 succession the archive did not have:**

| Date | Commander |
|---|---|
| Jan 1964 | Lt Col M. Grandelli |
| Jun 1964 | Lt Col John C. Hughes |
| Dec 1964 | Lt Col Robert K. Cunningham |
| Nov 1965 | Lt Col Charles M. Hanour |

Sergeant Major, June 1964: **Frank W. Ryan**.

**Subordinate units.** The battalion's composition changed across the two years:

- **1964:** the **117th**, **118th** and **120th** Aviation Companies, with the **53rd Medical
  Detachment (Helicopter)**. The **Utility Tactical Transport Helicopter Co (UTT)** appears
  operating alongside the 118th in January 1964 and in a March 1964 awards list beside 145th
  men — worth noting, because UTT is the archive's other candidate for Larry (#57).
- **Nov 1965:** the **6th Aviation Platoon** ("Fangs") joins at Tan Son Nhut.
- **Dec 1965**, from the citation when Prime Minister Nguyen Cao Ky decorated the battalion: the
  **74th, 82d, 118th and 120th** Aviation Companies, plus **A Companies of the 92d and 504th**
  Aviation Battalions.

**That December 1965 list is the most useful single line in the sweep.** If Larry was in the
145th but not the 118th, his company is very likely one of these — and the 74th, 82d, 120th,
A/92d and A/504th have never been considered here at all.

## The 118th Aviation Company, same sweep

- **At Bien Hoa** throughout (Jan 1964, Nov 1965).
- **Air Mobile Light**, paired with the 117th; the **93d Medical Detachment** attached by Oct 1965.
- Commanders: **Maj David B. Hayes** (Mar 1964), **Maj Vincent L. McNeese** (Jun 1964).
- Flew psychological-warfare support for the 5th Vietnamese Division in 1964, once airlifting a
  loudspeaker truck into country too dangerous to drive it into.
- Lead element for most of the lifts into the seven-day **battle of Binh Gia** (Dec 1964–Jan 1965).
- An item of **13 June 1965** has the 118th flying through heavy Viet Cong fire to evacuate
  wounded. **That is the Đồng Xoài week**, the battle Larry says he listened to on the radio, and
  it is worth pulling in full — see "Next" below.

## Handling — read before writing any of this to `data/`

**Rule 6 applies.** The interview guide asks Larry, open, *"do you remember which battalion your
company belonged to?"* — and the whole value of that question is that he answers it cold. **Do
not put "145th Aviation Battalion", "Tan Son Nhut" as its location, or any of the company names
above into `title`, `description`, `researchNotes` or `summary` on any page he browses**, and do
not read them to him. If he produces "145th" himself, that is corroboration of a documented
fact. If we hand it to him, it is worth nothing, and this is the single question where that
matters most.
