# 118th AHC Thunderbird Facebook group — outreach post (draft)

**Where:** https://www.facebook.com/groups/119640688162391/ ("118th AHC Thunderbird")
**Status:** drafted 2026-08-13, not yet posted
**Purpose:** find veterans who remember the Núi Bà Đen relay, the call signs, or Larry
himself — and gracefully learn if he belonged to a sister unit instead (the group's
members overlap with the 145th CAB community).

---

Hello Thunderbirds — I'm the son of a Vietnam veteran, trying to reconstruct my father's
1964–65 tour, and this group may be one of the few places left where someone remembers
the details firsthand.

My father, Larry Hunnewell, was an Army radio operator in III Corps from about September
1964 to September 1965 (he left early — medevaced to Camp Zama in Japan with hepatitis).
He remembers his headquarters being at Tan Son Nhut, and he was detached for stretches to
the Special Forces camp on Nui Ba Den — the Black Virgin Mountain, near Tay Ninh —
standing radio watch and search-and-rescue standby near the old French fort.

Things he remembers that we're trying to match to a unit:

- Call signs: the SF camp was "Body Jab 42," his own search-and-rescue call sign was
  "Tay Ninh," and when he needed a ride out he called "Rosethorn 007" — and they came.
- People: a company secretary named Kahn; his bunk mate Ron Tototz (a Polish name — we're
  unsure of the spelling); a Special Forces Sgt. Lowry or Lowery up on the mountain.
- He has always said "118th" — which is what led me to you.

Does anyone remember the radio relay on Nui Ba Den in '64–'65, missions supporting the SF
camp there, any of those call signs, or my dad? Even a small detail would mean a great
deal — he's 82 and telling his stories now, and we're writing every one of them down. I'm
glad to share his photos from the mountain, including one of a rocket launcher being
rigged on the summit that some of you may find interesting. Thank you — and thank you all
for what you did.
