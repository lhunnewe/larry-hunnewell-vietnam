# "Saigon center" and Army flight following, 1964–65 — research findings (2026-08-16)

Deep dive for issue #57, building on Larry's 2026-08-15 testimony: he was a **flight
controller** (VN-0011), worked twelve-hour night shifts alone in the radio van (VN-0015),
and from Núi Bà Đen ran **two FM nets to "Saigon center"** with his mountain station "the
only annex of it" (VN-0059). Everything below is web-research **lead material**, not
established fact, unless marked otherwise.

## Finding 1 — "AOD" is real period vocabulary (changes the issue #10 picture)

The family memory is "118th **AOD — Aviation Operations Detachment**." The 2026-08-13
sweep correctly found **no numbered unit** with that designation in Vietnam — but the
*term* turns out to be real Army vocabulary: Army air traffic control "began in 1956 as
the **Army Aviation Operation Detachment (AAOD)** under the Continental Army Command," with
the mission "to assist aviation units to operate at night and in bad weather conditions"
(Wikipedia, "Army Air Traffic Services Command"). Aviation operating detachments were the
TOE vehicle for exactly Larry's job — flight-following and airfield operations teams.

**Implication:** "AOD" need not be a mishearing of "AML" (the 2026-08-13 resolution). The
alternative reading is now at least as strong: Larry served in or with an **aviation
operations detachment/element**, and "118th" is the number his memory attached to it —
possibly from the 118th Aviation Company his net supported daily. Both readings stay open.

## Finding 2 — the institutional successor confirms the job description

The **125th Air Traffic Control Company** (later 125th ATC Battalion (Prov.)) is the
documented later embodiment of Larry's job, per the VHPA museum unit page
(museum.vhpa.org/misc/125atc/125atc.shtml):

- mission: "**non-divisional Air Traffic Control and Flight Following for all US Army
  Aviation**";
- headquarters at **Tan Son Nhut** (later Bien Hoa);
- four platoons assigned by Corps area;
- "Flight following services via UHF, VHF, **and FM**" and "**Remote relay radio
  operations**" — including III Corps **mountain relay sites** (Vo Dat mountain, Song Be).

The 125th's documented material is 1968+; in Larry's 1964–65 window this company was not
yet operating in-country. So his FM flight-following net with a one-man mountain annex was
the **pre-institutional, ad-hoc version** of exactly this mission — which is why it appears
in no unit lineage page. (Same gap pattern as the Núi Bà Đen relay: the famous 121st Signal
relay starts Feb 1966; Larry's window falls in the undocumented gap before it.)

## Finding 3 — what "Saigon center" was and was not

Three candidate identities were tested:

1. **"Paris Control" (the issue #57 hypothesis) — now assessed UNLIKELY.** Paris Control is
   documented as the **USAF GCI (radar) site at Saigon/Tan Son Nhut**, one of a family of
   sites: Paddy (Binh Thuy), Panama (Da Nang), Pyramid (Ban Me Thuot), Port Call (Nha
   Trang). They "did advisory radar flight following" for aircraft flying TAC VFR
   (vietnamairlift.com/tacvfr.html, C-123 pilot memoir). But these were Air Force radar
   facilities working UHF/VHF, chiefly for fixed-wing. Larry's net ran on **FM ("both of
   the fox mikes")** — the Army tactical band — and he calls the facility "our own radio."
   An Army FM net annexed on Núi Bà Đen does not fit a USAF GCI site.
2. **Civil Saigon ACC** (the ICAO area control center at Tan Son Nhut) — controlled the
   airways; no FM, no Army annexes. Not it. (Worth remembering only because "Saigon
   Center" was its literal radio name for airline traffic, which may be where the *phrase*
   was in the air.)
3. **The Army flight-following net centered at Tan Son Nhut — the fit.** In 1964–65 Army
   flight following in III Corps ran through the aviation battalion structure. The **145th
   Aviation Battalion had its headquarters at Tan Son Nhut through Larry's whole tour**
   (LTC Charles M. Honour Jr. assumed command "at Tan Son Nhut Air Base on 24 June 1965,"
   145thcab.com; the HQ moved to Bien Hoa only in 1966). Its subordinate companies — the
   118th (Bien Hoa), the 120th and UTT/68th (Tan Son Nhut) — flew III Corps, the exact
   area of Larry's grid board (VN-0015).

**Working assessment (hypothesis, do not render):** "Saigon center" = the Army
flight-following / flight operations center at Tan Son Nhut, most plausibly operated by
**HHC, 145th Aviation Battalion** or the aviation-operations element of U.S. Army Support
Command Vietnam co-located there. This would make Larry's "headquarters company at Tan Son
Nhut" literally the **Headquarters (and Headquarters) Company of an aviation battalion** —
a unit of roughly the ~110 men he remembers, full of radio operators, some cleared for
special traffic, supporting (and remembered through) the 118th. The 1st Cavalry
association remains unexplained under every candidate.

## What would settle it

- **145th Avn Bn ORLLs / unit history, 1964–65** (NARA RG 472; Texas Tech Vietnam Archive):
  a battalion flight-operations center, its FM nets, and any Núi Bà Đen outstation would
  appear in operational reports. This is the single highest-value document pull.
- **USASCV aviation/signal operational reports 1964–65** (Texas Tech): same question from
  the theater side.
- **Morning reports for HHC 145th Avn Bn, Sep 1964 – Sep 1965** (NPRC): would show
  Hunnewell, Weaver, Jones, Parr, a Tototz-like name, Major Lewis, Captain Guegler.
- **TSNA (Tan Son Nhut Association, tsna.org)**: veteran association specifically for Tan
  Son Nhut tenants; guestbook and member search could surface 1964–65 Army flight
  controllers who knew the net's name.
- **Ask Larry (open questions only, in the interview guide):** which battalion his company
  belonged to; what other outfits flew from Tan Son Nhut and how their aircraft were
  marked.

## 2026-08-23 — Larry names the 125th himself, unprompted, and contradicts Finding 2

**Provenance, which decides whether this counts at all:** Larry volunteered "125th" with no
prompting. Per his son, he said it in the car on the way to the VA earlier the same day,
unasked and out of nowhere, and it was typed into the Memories box afterwards
(`data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18118728.json`). The interview rule for this topic —
*do NOT name any unit to him* — was not broken. This is his own recall, not an echo of the
candidate list in this file.

**What he said:** "Another unit called the **125th air traffic controllers was moving in
after his unit to take their place**. He only had [a few] days left before his ets."

**What that does to Finding 2.** This file argued that the 125th's documented material is
1968-and-later, that the company "was not yet operating in-country" in 1964–65, and that
Larry's net was therefore the *pre-institutional* version of the mission — an argument the
unresolved ledger repeats. Larry places the 125th arriving **as he was leaving**, i.e.
around September 1965. Three ways to reconcile, none established:

1. **The lineage date is about the company's formal constitution, not its presence.** An
   advance party or a predecessor detachment could arrive well before the date a lineage
   page records. This would leave the substance of Finding 2 intact while making its
   "not yet in-country" phrasing wrong.
2. **Larry has the number right and the date drifted.** He may be remembering a later
   handover he heard about rather than witnessed.
3. **He is retro-fitting a designation learned afterwards.** Possible for any veteran — but
   weakened here by the unprompted, out-of-context way it surfaced, and by his tying it to a
   specific personal fact (his own remaining service time) rather than to a narrative.

**Why this matters more than the usual candidate:** a named successor unit is a documentary
handle. If the 125th (or a predecessor) took over a flight-following mission near Saigon in
late 1965, *its* records name what it replaced — which is Larry's outfit. That is a route to
the unit question that does not depend on finding Larry in a roster.

**Next query:** the 125th ATC company's lineage and honors certificate and any 1965–66 unit
history, for an arrival or activation date and for what mission it assumed from whom.

## 2026-08-23 — "the property of 18th aviation"

Asked where the radio van stood, Larry answered: "It was over in **the property of 18th
aviation**. They were huge. They had probably about **300 helicopters including sky
cranes**. It was **before the first cav aviation**."
(`data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18118818.json`)

**The 18th has never appeared in this archive before** — every unit pass has weighed the
118th, 145th, 120th, UTT/68th and 125th. Three readings:

1. **A landlord, not a parent.** He says the van stood *on their property*, which is a
   tenancy claim, not a chain of command. A large aviation organisation hosting a small
   flight-following element fits everything else he has said about being attached rather
   than assigned.
2. **A mis-numbering of the 118th**, which he has said before and which sounds close.
3. **A real 18th**, to be checked against period aviation organisations of that size.

"Sky cranes" (CH-54 Tarhe) is a checkable detail with a documented in-country date, and
"before the first cav aviation" is a sequencing claim that bears directly on the remembered
1st Cavalry association in issue #10 — his outfit was there *first*, which would explain an
association without requiring him to have served in the division.

**Next query:** which aviation organisations near Saigon held ~300 airframes in 1964–65, and
when CH-54s first appear in-country.
