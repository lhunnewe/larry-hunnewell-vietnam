# Units arriving at Tân Sơn Nhứt and Biên Hòa, May–August 1965

**Written:** 2026-09-12. For **ledger #11 / issue #59** (the bathroom shooting), plus the
"18th Aviation" lead that ledger #2 lists as NOT CHECKED.
**Method:** open-web desk search only (web search and page fetches; no browser session, no paid
archives). Every URL below was accessed **2026-09-12**.
**Provenance:** desk search by Claude Opus 5 for Larry's son. No new testimony. **Research file
only.** Nothing here goes into a rendered field (see "Handling" at the bottom).

## Read this first

1. **The base is a hypothesis.** Larry calls it "Ton Son Nhut". Tân Sơn Nhứt is the leading
   reading, but the unit lead (118th Aviation Company) lived at Biên Hòa, and the working
   hypothesis that he was at 145th Aviation Battalion HQ at Tân Sơn Nhứt
   (`research/units/145th-avn-bn-tan-son-nhut.md`) is not proven. So this file gives **two
   lists**, one per base, and weights neither.
2. **These lists do not identify the shooter or his unit.** They only list documented
   arrivals that fit Larry's time frame. Nothing below links any unit to the shooting.
3. **What is being tested.** At VN-0017, which he dates "probably July of 1965", Larry said
   (transcribed, `data/recollections/giscus-vn-0017-c18415788.json`): *"The other Unit is
   somewhere in the background, the guy that did the shooting in the bathroom. They had just
   arrived maybe a week or so."* His son says "just arrived" is when the other unit got
   there, about a week before the photograph. The photo date is Larry's estimate. So the
   window used here, roughly **1 May – 15 August 1965**, is wide on purpose. The earlier
   account (`giscus-stories-c18035411.json`, a **paraphrase**) says "a different
   company / unit" and "Airforce police showed up". Both wordings are his son's typing, so
   "company" and "soldier" should not be read as proof the man was Army.
4. **The date tension stays open.** Larry also dated the shooting to February–March 1965.
   This file does not resolve that.

## Which candidates are strongest, and why (no identification)

**What would make a good match.** A unit that (a) reached the same base about a week before a
photograph Larry places in "probably July" 1965, (b) had its area within sight of his hooch
("somewhere in the background"), and (c) had men who could plausibly be in his company's
latrine/shower at night.

**If the base is Tân Sơn Nhứt:**

- **481st TFS (USAF), 21–29 June 1965.** This is the **only unit-sized arrival found at Tân Sơn
  Nhứt itself** inside the window, and its date sits "a week or so" before an early-July
  photograph. **Against:** it was an Air Force fighter squadron on temporary duty, living in
  the USAF area around the flight line. No source reached says where it was billeted, and
  nothing suggests its men used an Army headquarters company's latrine. **One point not to
  over-read:** Air Force police taking the statement fits a USAF suspect, but it fits just as
  well an Army suspect on a USAF-policed base.
- **12th Aviation Group HQ, August 1965.** An aviation group headquarters arriving to command
  the 145th and its sister battalions is **the most natural neighbour for an Army aviation
  battalion headquarters company**, if it set up at Tân Sơn Nhứt. **Against:** its arrival
  station is not confirmed; "August" fits "probably July" loosely at best; and Larry left in
  late August 1965. **The day of arrival and the station are the facts to find.**
- **Temporary-duty rotations**, as a category rather than a unit. The 118th's association
  site records 90-day TDY door gunners from the 25th Infantry Division
  (`research/units/callsigns-and-units.md`). A fresh TDY contingent is "just arrived" by
  nature and gets housed in whatever hooches are free. No source reached puts such a
  contingent at Tân Sơn Nhứt in June–July 1965. Recorded as a line to pursue, not a
  candidate.
- USARV (20 July) and the 6250th CSG (8 July) are **redesignations, not arrivals**. Nobody
  "just arrived" with them, so they are listed only to show they were ruled out.

**If the base is Biên Hòa:**

- **2d Brigade, 1st Infantry Division, about 15–17 July 1965.** This is **the best timing fit
  anywhere**: several thousand newly arrived Army soldiers reaching the airfield in mid-July.
  **Against:** its base camp was about 3 km southeast of the airfield (S22), so on a separate
  site. Sharing a latrine with a headquarters company *on* the air base would take a reason
  (a work detail, a visit, drinking at a club), and no source gives one.
- **173d Airborne Brigade, 3–7 May 1965.** A large Army arrival, camped "on the northeast
  perimeter of the air base" (S19), but **two months early** for "a week or so" before a July
  photograph. It fits only if the photograph is earlier than Larry thinks.
- **416th TFS (23 June) and 307th TFS (13 July), USAF.** Timing fits. The same objections as
  the 481st apply.

**A structural finding, and a caution about it.** In the sources reached, Tân Sơn Nhứt shows
**no Army company- or battalion-sized unit arriving between May and mid-August 1965**. Biên
Hòa shows a stream of them. That is **not evidence for Biên Hòa**:

- the 145th hypothesis has Larry at a headquarters whose neighbours arrived in small pieces;
- small detachments rarely appear in unit-arrival lists;
- Stanton's order of battle, the one source that lists arrivals down to separate companies,
  **could not be reached** (see negatives).

It does mean the Tân Sơn Nhứt list is **incomplete in exactly the size of unit that matters
most**.

## The 18th Aviation Company: where it was in 1964–65

**Answer, with sources:** the **18th Aviation Company (Fixed Wing Light Transport), U-1A Otter**,
had its **headquarters at Nha Trang** and **detachments around the country, including Saigon
(Tân Sơn Nhứt)**, from 1962 until **September 1965**. That month the newly arrived 54th Aviation
Company took over the Saigon and Cần Thơ stations.

- Unit statement of service (18/54 Otternest association): boarded USNS *Core* 15 Jan 1962;
  **"Arrived 6 February 1962 in Saigon, Vietnam"**; inactivated Qui Nhơn 15 April 1971.
  Commanders in Larry's window: Maj Raymond E. Dickens (from 30 Dec 1964) and **Maj Paul S.
  Walker (from 20 June 1965)**. MUC "Vietnam 1964–1965". (S26)
- Unit members' stories (same association, the March 1962 section): "Company headquarters was
  established at the **Nha Trang airfield** and provided logistics support to the three
  detachments located at Da Nang (I Corps), Pleiku (II Corps) and **Saigon (III Corps)**". One
  scheduled flight "departed the **Saigon-Tan Son Nhut airfield**". (S27)
- VHPA museum page: "The HQ was in Nha Trang, but the company had airplanes scattered throughout
  the country-Danang, Pleiku, Banmethuot and Saigon." (S28)
- A 114th Aviation Company pilot who arrived 19 Aug 1964: "directed to check in at the **18th
  Aviation Company (U-1 'Otter' …) operations at Tan Son Nhut** and hitch a ride to Nha Trang".
  (S29)
- 14th Aviation Battalion history (VHPA): the battalion HQ arrived in Saigon 14 Oct 1964, moved
  to Nha Trang 21 Oct 1964, and was "originally composed of … **18th Aviation Company**, …".
  (S30) Tolson's *Airmobility* has "the 14th Aviation Battalion at Nha Trang … one fixed-wing
  Otter company" as 1964 closed. It also places "the 18th Aviation Company (U-1A Otters)" in
  the Aviation Support Battalion (Provisional) at an earlier point that the page does not date
  clearly. (S31)
- 54th Aviation Company history (association): the 54th put "the 1st Platoon at **Tan Son Nhut
  airfield**, Saigon … and the 2nd Platoon in Can Tho … These two bases had been serviced by the
  18th Aviation Company up to this, so effectively the 54th took over from the 18th". Its first
  Otter, "one of the 8 Otters transferred from the 18th", was taken on charge **27 Sept
  1965**. (S14)

**What this does and does not do for Larry's "18th aviation":**

- It **confirms a real 18th Aviation Company with a presence at Tân Sơn Nhứt throughout his
  tour**, which his son reports he said as "18th", not "118th". It stays a separate lead from
  the 118th, as the ledger asks.
- It **does not match his description of the 18th**: "huge … about 300 helicopters including
  sky cranes". The 18th flew **fixed-wing** Otters. Its 1959 strength was "22 officers and 48
  enlisted men" (S27), and in 1965 it transferred eight Otters to the 54th (S14). CH-54 sky
  cranes came to Vietnam in 1965 with the 1st Cavalry Division's 478th Aviation Company
  (S32), at An Khê, not Saigon. So either the number, the description, or both belong to
  something else. This file does not choose.
- The "property" was probably a Saigon detachment's flight-line area, **not a company
  headquarters**. That is an inference from the sources above, not a finding.
- **Do not confuse it with the 18th Corps Aviation Company ("Green Delta")**: provisional
  1 June 1971, redesignated 15 Aug 1971 in the Delta, "the largest Aviation company in Vietnam
  … over 50 aircraft, plus 500 officers and enlisted men" (S33). Wrong years, but it is the
  only "18th" found that was actually huge. If Larry's detail was absorbed from later
  reading, this is where it could have come from. Speculation, noted only so the collision is
  recognised.

## Side findings (for other ledger entries)

- **"12th Avn Grp" in the Coffelt chain for 1964 deaths is anachronistic.** The 12th Aviation
  Group was **activated 18 June 1965** (S8). The chain `118th Avn Co → 145th Avn Bn → 12th Avn
  Grp` in `118th-avn-co-losses-oct-nov-1964.md` therefore normalises the group level exactly as
  it does "1st Avn Bde". Company and battalion stay reliable; **the group level should not be
  quoted for 1964.**
- **145th Avn Bn change of command.** The 145thcab.com history: "On **24 June 1965** LTC Charles
  M. Honour Jr. assumed command of the battalion at Tan Son Nhut Air Base." (S25) The archive's
  Stars and Stripes table has "Nov 1965 | Lt Col Charles M. Hanour". That is probably the same
  man seen later rather than a later change of command. Spelling and date conflict, not
  reconciled. The same page says the battalion HQ "moved from Tan Son Nhut to Bien Hoa City"
  in March (from context, 1966), which agrees with `flight-following-1964-65.md`.
- **The Okinawa sentence does not narrow anything.** The 173d came from Okinawa (S17), but
  confinement on Okinawa was a Pacific-wide destination, so it carries no unit signal. Noted so
  nobody mistakes it for a link.

## Interview questions (open; no unit names, places or dates; rule 6)

For the guide, if the son wants them. Each is phrased so any unit detail must come from Larry.

- "Tell me about the other outfit that came in near your hooch. What did those men do?"
- "How could you tell they were a different outfit from yours?"
- "Where did they sleep and wash? Did they use your company's latrine, or their own?"
- "Did you ever find out what outfit the man with the pistol belonged to? How did you hear?"
- "What else was going on around the base about the time of the shooting?"
