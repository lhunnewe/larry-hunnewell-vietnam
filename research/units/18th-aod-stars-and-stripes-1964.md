## Document 1 — Pacific Stars and Stripes, Saturday 24 October 1964, archive page 29 (printed 19)

- Viewer: `https://starsandstripes.newspaperarchive.com/pacific-stars-and-stripes/1964-10-24/page-29/`
- imageID `141640697`. Article occupies the right ~40% of the page; photo and caption below it.
- Kicker **"18TH AOD"**, headline **"They Keep Eye On Lost Planes"**. Dateline **TAN SON NHUT,
  Vietnam (IO)** — an Army information-office release.

What it establishes, read in full (paraphrased; short quotes only):

1. **A unit with Larry's abbreviation existed, at his base, doing his job.** The "18th Aviation
   Operating Det. (AOD)" of the U.S. Army Support Command, Vietnam, based at Tan Son Nhut,
   commanded by **Maj. William D. Phillips** of Norman, Okla., ran the Army's nationwide
   **flight-following radio monitor system** — a record of the location of every Army aircraft.
2. **The system:** the Army Flight Operations System (AFOS), the outgrowth of a study headed by
   Col. John L. Klingenhagen, deputy CO of Support Command; under Maj. Gen. Delk M. Oden, USASCV.
   **Thirteen radio relay stations** "from locations near the 17th parallel in the north to the
   outposts in the Delta"; **three Flight Operations Centers (FOC)** beside the Air Force Tactical
   Operations Centers at **Saigon, Pleiku and Can Tho**. The relay stations are "operated by
   members of the 18th AOD," augmented by air traffic control men from the aviation units.
3. **What the FOC does when an aircraft is overdue:** alerts the nearest unit, scrambles standby
   aircraft, or calls USAF Search and Rescue. Worked example: a USAF Skyraider downed near a Delta
   village in Vinh Binh Province; the Can Tho FOC heard the call and the pilot was picked up in
   twelve minutes.
4. **"Primary coordination … is accomplished by the Saigon Headquarters of the 18th AOD,"**
   working with the Air Force Air Operations Center and the Vietnamese civil aviation directorate.
5. **Photograph and caption:** two air traffic controllers and their supervisor manning the Army
   Flight Operations System "in a specially designed III Corps Flight Operations Center van at Tan
   Son Nhut Airfield near Saigon." **From left: 1st Lt. Douglas L. Hanna, officer-in-charge; Sgt.
   Rene C. LeFrancois; and PFC Tommy R. Chapman.** (USA Photo.) The picture shows men at desks under
   rows of slatted status boards.

## Document 2 — Pacific Stars and Stripes, Monday 25 January 1965, archive page 20

- Viewer: `…/pacific-stars-and-stripes/1965-01-25/page-20/`, imageID `141661633` (same column also

  coordinating all Army aircraft in III Corps (officer-in-charge Capt. Richard K. Zimmerman; CWO2

- So, **inside Larry's tour**, the same detachment (expanded here as "Air Operations Det.") ran a
  Flight Following Center in the room next to the 145th Battalion's control desk in Saigon.

## Assessment

- **Larry's unit was the 18th Aviation Operating (Air Operations) Detachment, USASCV, at Tan Son
  Nhut — strongly-supported**, not confirmed. What would confirm it: his name on the detachment's
  morning reports or orders (issue #27, NPRC), or the OMPF (#19). The "118th" is best read as his
  memory fusing "18th AOD" with the 118th Aviation Company, the best-known unit on the net — the
  2026-08-16 reading in `flight-following-1964-65.md` ("AOD is real period vocabulary") is borne
  out, and the 2026-08-13 conclusion that "no unit designated … AOD existed" is **overturned** for
  the 18th.
- **The base identification (ledger #3) is strengthened:** a Tan Son Nhut unit whose members Larry
  names. Still his testimony plus a unit match, not his own paper.
- **The names are identifications of men, not of photographs.** Nothing here says any of the three
  is in Larry's slides.

## Tensions — recorded, not reconciled

1. **Hanna.** Larry: a pilot, "19 or 20," cross-eyed, "killed in 1968 Kontom," "I think he was 1st
   cavalary." **DCAS full-file check 2026-09-12 (PalmBeachPost `vnusreport.txt`, all 58k US dead):
   no Douglas Hanna.** All 13 Hanna/Hannah entries were read; none is a Douglas, and the only 1968
   officer is a Marine 2nd Lt. (Samuel J. Hannah, infantry). So *this* Hanna did not die in Vietnam. Either the death belongs to
   another man (the VHPA/DCAS WO1 Gary W. Hanna, KIA 31 Jan 1968, 1st Cav, is already ruled out on
   age), or the memory of his death is wrong, or Larry's Hanna is not this officer. An
   officer-in-charge of an FOC van is not necessarily a pilot.
2. **Chapman's rank.** PFC in October 1964; Larry's Chapman is an E7, and the VN-0024 man is an
   older sergeant with a graying crew cut. A PFC cannot be an E7 within a year. Either two
   Chapmans, or Larry's rank is wrong, or the VN-0024 man is not this Chapman. Open.
3. **Timing.** The caption date, 24 Oct 1964, falls in what Larry describes as his first two
   months at Nha Trang. The photo may predate his joining the van crew; the men would still have
   been there when he came.
4. **"Aviation Operating" vs "Air Operations."** The two documents expand AOD differently. Both are
   Army information releases; neither is an order. Larry's own "Aviation Operations" sits between.
