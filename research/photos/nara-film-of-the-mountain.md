# Film of Núi Bà Đen in the National Archives — links, dates, and a caution

Found in the free catalog pass of 2026-09-01
(`../records-request/nara-catalog-pass-2026-09-01.md`). Kept here as its own file so the
links are easy to find again.

A search for `"Nui Ba Den"` on <https://catalog.archives.gov> returns **199 records, 175
available online**, of which **9 are moving images and 4 of those are viewable now**.

## The films, with links

anything Larry says. He arrived in Vietnam about September 1964 and left by **medical
evacuation in early September 1965**, so his year is 1964–65 and nothing outside it is
evidence about his service.

In the Army items (RG 111, *Filmed News Releases*) the `-65`, `-68`, `-69`, `-70` suffix in
the local ID is the year, which is what dates rows 2–5.

## Are these his mountain?

**Yes and no, and the distinction matters.**

Yes: it is the same mountain, the same province, and in several cases the same summit
position — the place he manned a radio.

No: **every one of the four Núi Bà Đen films postdates him.** He left Vietnam by medical
evacuation in **early September 1965**. The earliest of these was produced in **December
1967**, two years and three months later, and the others are 1968, 1969 and 1970.

That gap is not cosmetic. In 1964–65 the summit held a **half A-team — one officer and five
enlisted men — and a radio relay** (`../places/nui-ba-den-locations.md`). By 1967–70 it was a
substantial American signal installation with a post exchange flown in by helicopter, which
is literally the subject of film 2. The mountain in these films is a place his mountain
became after he left. **They cannot corroborate anything he says**, and nothing in them
should be treated as showing his camp.

**The exception is row 5**, and it is why that row exists. *Seabees in Vietnam, Bến Sỏi, Tây
Ninh Province* is dated **1965** — his year — and Bến Sỏi is **Detachment A-321, under the
same B-32 that ran Núi Bà Đen**. Not his mountain, but his province, his year, and the
neighbouring camp of the same B-detachment. Nobody has watched it. It is the most
interesting item this pass turned up.

## Should they be watched with him?

That is the family's decision, not a research one. Three things worth knowing before making
it:

1. **Watch them first, without him.** They have not been viewed by anyone on this project.
   Nobody should sit an 86-year-old down in front of unviewed war footage of a place that
   carries what this one carries for him.
2. **One open question would be spoiled, and it is a good one.** The roster-rock question —
   *"do you remember these names painted on the rocks?"* (VN-0035, VN-0133) — has not been
   asked yet, and two of those names are now matched to real officers
   (`../people/nui-ba-den-roster-names.md`). If any of these films shows painted rocks or
   names on the summit, showing him first would contaminate it permanently. **Ask the rock
   question before any screening.** The other probes he answered cold on 2026-09-01 are
   already banked and cannot be spoiled now.
3. **Frame it honestly if it happens.** Not "here is your camp" — it is not. "This is the
   mountain a few years after you left, and here is what the Army built on it." Anything he
   says while watching is still worth writing down, but it is **commentary on a later film**,
   not testimony about 1965, and should be recorded as such.
