# The CBC documentary — first research pass, 2026-09-01

**Claim:** Larry says a Canadian Broadcasting Corporation crew filmed where he was for about
two weeks, that they asked him questions on camera, that they all ate together, and that
photographs of the crew are among his own — "I believe it was done in '64 maybe around
Christmas time." Recorded as paraphrase in
`data/recollections/2026-09-01-phone-cbc-crew.json`. Ledger #19, issue **#69**.

**Status of this pass:** one strong candidate found and viewable, but **it does not fit the
dates or the places**. Recorded as a candidate to test, not an identification.

---

## The candidate: *The Mills of the Gods: Viet Nam* (1965)

The one substantial CBC Vietnam documentary of the period, and it is famous:

- **Series:** *Document*, CBC's documentary strand, companion to the newsmagazine *This Hour
  Has Seven Days*
- **Aired:** **5 December 1965**, CBC Television
- **Director/producer:** Beryl Fox
- **Camera:** Erik Durschmied, with Tim Page taking over when Durschmied fell ill
- **Narrator:** Bernard B. Fall
- **Award:** George Polk Award for Best Television Documentary, 1966
- **Filming:** "late 1965," about **three weeks**

**It is viewable, free, right now** — on CBC's own player at
<https://www.cbc.ca/player/play/2676694740>, and in full on YouTube at
<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EIZr_ddDuEE>.

Note on rights, since the question was asked: *viewable* is not *public domain*. CBC holds
the copyright and streams it themselves; watching it costs nothing, but using footage in
anything published would need CBC's permission.

## Why it probably is not Larry's crew

Two mismatches, and they are not small:

1. **Dates.** Filming was late 1965 over about three weeks; the film aired 5 December 1965.
   Larry was **medevaced to Japan in early September 1965**. A late-1965 shoot would have
   reached Vietnam after he had left the country. His own date, "'64 maybe around Christmas
   time," is a year earlier still — and while that is his least certain detail, it points
   away from this film, not toward it.
2. **Places.** The locations recorded for it are the **Mekong Delta**, Vietnamese villages,
   and scenes aboard the USNS *General Simon B. Buckner* — IV Corps and afloat. Larry served
   in **III Corps**: Tân Sơn Nhứt and Núi Bà Đen, in Tây Ninh. No account of the film
   mentions Tây Ninh, Núi Bà Đen, Tân Sơn Nhứt, Special Forces, or an aviation unit.

What does fit is only the shape of the thing: a CBC crew, a documentary strand, and a shoot
of roughly two to three weeks.

## What keeps the thread alive

**Erik Durschmied covered Vietnam for CBC from 1961 to 1970.** *Mills of the Gods* was not
his first time in the country, only his most celebrated. A CBC crew filming in Vietnam in
1964 or early 1965 is therefore entirely plausible; it simply has not been documented in this
pass. CBC's Vietnam output of 1964–65 beyond the one famous film is not well indexed on the
open web, which is exactly the kind of gap an archive request closes.

## An alternative worth testing — and not asserting

There is an innocent way "CBC" could be the wrong three letters, and it should be written
down before anybody spends money on the assumption that it is right:

**Morley Safer was a Canadian who worked for the CBC before joining CBS**, and became CBS's
Saigon bureau chief in 1965. American network crews in Vietnam in 1964–65 sometimes carried
Canadian correspondents and Canadian cameramen. A Canadian voice at the table, sixty-one
years ago, is an easy and entirely honest route to remembering the network as CBC.

**This does not override Larry.** His word is CBC, his son confirms he has said CBC
consistently over the years, and this archive does not correct his testimony from a
plausible alternative. It is recorded so that if the CBC trail runs dry, CBS and the other
American networks are the next place to look rather than a dead end.

## Next, in order

1. **Ask Larry to describe the crew before showing him anything.** How many men, what
   equipment, did they say where they were from, did he hear accents, was there a reporter on
   camera as well as a cameraman, and where were they filming — the mountain, Tân Sơn Nhứt,
   or somewhere else? A description given cold can be matched; a description given after
   watching a film cannot.
2. **Then, and only then, consider watching *Mills of the Gods* with him.** It is free and
   twenty-two minutes of it would be recognisable terrain even if it is the wrong film. But
   the order matters: his own account first.
3. **Library and Archives Canada** holds CBC News Library film. Their film, video and sound
   database has CBC News Library records; a request for CBC Vietnam material of 1964–65 is
   the Canadian equivalent of the Texas Tech request and should be written the same way —
   naming programme strand, year, and subject.
4. **CBC's own archives** licence and search their holdings; a reference enquiry asking what
   CBC shot in Vietnam in 1964 and early 1965, and whether crew lists survive, is one email.
5. **The photographs**, if they are ever found, would settle it in a second — a crew
   photograph with equipment markings, or a face matched to Durschmied or another CBC
   cameraman. They are not among the 157 scans
   (`../photos/scan-reconciliation-2026-09-01.md`).
