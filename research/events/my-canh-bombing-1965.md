## What he said, and how it arrived

Unprompted. The survey had put a question about the bombing in the observation record for VN-0079
on 2026-08-09 ("Do you remember when it was bombed in June 1965 — where were you when you
heard?") and it was never asked. He got there himself from a picture of a different street:

> To the left is the Presidential Palace and to the right is the Mekong River and a floating
> restaurant where I almost got blown up. The floating restaurant was called the MyChan
> (pronounced "My-Can") floating restaurant, it was like a barge. They had a claymore mine and
> when it fires, it fires 700 buckshot bullets in a curve and it's deadly. I was in front of the
> restaurant and a friend named Lopez invited me to the restaurant called the Peacock and when we
> entered the Peacock the MyChan blew up. It was like a guardian angel. Lopez was my pal
> throughout the cub scouts. Lopez was part of the Marines and just showed up at this moment to
> invite me to the Peacock. It was a miracle he showed up. The next day i took pictures of the
> MyChan restaurant of it blown up. The picture may be missing though.

Three other comments the same sitting place him at the restaurant as a regular (VN-0072, "we
always ate here", Ron Tototz across the table), name it from the quay (VN-0079), and put the
camera on its deck looking at the naval quay (VN-0089).

## What the documents say

The attack is one of the best-recorded of the war. Bundy's telegram to Johnson the next morning
(FRUS 1964–68 vol. III doc. 20, `sources/frus-1964-68-v03-d20`) gives **44 dead — 27 Vietnamese,
13 U.S., 2 French, 1 Swiss, 1 Filipino — and 81 wounded**. Every reconstruction agrees on the
mechanism: **two command-detonated directional mines**, the first fired into the dining deck, the
second placed on the shore to cover the gangway and the crowd that ran for it. Time: evening
dinner hour. Larry's "claymore mine … 700 buckshot bullets in a curve" is the weapon exactly.

Contemporary press coverage exists in bulk (AP/UPI wire, 26 June 1965; Stars and Stripes Pacific
would have run it within days). **Not yet read.** A pass through the 26–28 June 1965 wire copy for
the names of the restaurants nearby — is there a "Peacock"? — and for any account of a soldier
photographing the wreck the next day is the obvious next step, and cheap.

## Why it matters more than most stories

1. **It is the only day of his tour he fixes to a documented calendar date by being there.**
   Everything else in the chronology is his memory of a month, or a derivation. This is a
   Friday evening, and he was in Saigon.
2. **It bounds the mountain stints.** Đồng Xoài was 9–10 June 1965 and he says he heard it from
   the radio mountain. Sixteen days later he is in the city. A stint of "a week or 10 days,
   sometimes two back to back" (VN-0044, 2026-09-02) fits with room to spare; it also says he was
   not on a double stint that ran through late June.
3. **It names a friend who is findable.** Lopez: same cub scout pack as Larry, which means
   Inglewood, California, early 1950s; a Marine in Saigon in June 1965. Before the ground
   buildup reached the capital, Marines in Saigon were overwhelmingly the **Marine Security
   Guard detachment at the Embassy** and **MACV headquarters guard** — small units with
   rosters. That is a hypothesis for the search, not for Larry; per rule 6 it lives here and not
   on his person page. Ask him only "Tell me about Lopez."
4. **It names a missing photograph — the first concrete one.** The scan reconciliation
   (`../photos/scan-reconciliation-2026-09-01.md`) established that the lab numbered every frame
   it received E001–E157 without a gap, so nothing was lost *after* the family handed the slides
   over. If he photographed the wreck, that slide left the set before scanning. Tier-1 probe #5
   ("the missing photographs") now has a specific thing to ask about — and he raised it himself,
   so asking does not prime.

## Two things to keep apart

- **This is not the VN-0120 explosion.** On 2026-09-02 he described a bar near "the Palace" with
  a throat wound he bandaged with his T-shirt, and on 2026-09-03 dated it to the **US Embassy
  bombing of 30 March 1965**, a block from the Papillon (ledger #23). Two bombings, three months
  apart, both his. Nothing in the Mỹ Cảnh account mentions wounded he treated; nothing in the
  Embassy account mentions Lopez.
- **Nothing documentary puts Larry at the Mỹ Cảnh.** He is in no casualty list (he was not
  hurt), no press account names him, and his own photograph of the aftermath is gone. The event
  is `confirmed`; his presence is `unverified-recollection`, and that is how the records are
  rated.
