# The Market explosion — which market, which day (not yet identified)

**Research note, 2026-09-12.** Ledger #36, issue #154. Source of the testimony:
`data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18415576.json` (transcribed, typed by his son as Larry spoke).
Related: `data/drawings/VD-0003.json` (the circled M), `data/recollections/2026-09-05-drawing-market-m.json`
(paraphrase), `giscus-vn-0120-c18259873.json` (the throat wound near the Palace),
`2026-09-03-phone-tu-do-explosion.json` (the Embassy, "about a block away"), and
`research/events/my-canh-bombing-1965.md`. Every source below was accessed 2026-09-12 from the open
web. Nothing here has been written to `data/`.

**Result in one line: no documented explosion at a Saigon market between September 1964 and
August 1965 has been found.** The closest documentary matches each fail on at least two of his
details. What the search did turn up is one strong overlap with a bombing he has already described
elsewhere, and that overlap is recorded below and not merged.

## What he said

> The Market Explosion story
> I was probably 6 or 8 bars away from the explosion. I heard the explosion and somebody, as i was
> running down somebody in English said bicycle bomb. There were no Americans hurt. I tried to stop
> the bleeding of a women by compressing the blood in and then someone else took over of
> Vietnamese. It was horrific, women and children were hurt bad. I didn't see any men there. There
> were 3 or 4 Americans running down with me, I didn't know any of them.

The details to test against documents:

1. a market (the circled M on VD-0003, at the head of the wide band, the end near "TO BASE / A PORT", away from the river at the bottom of the sheet);
2. he was in bar country ("6 or 8 bars away") and ran to it;
3. an English-speaker called it a bicycle bomb;
4. no Americans hurt;
5. women and children badly hurt, no men seen;
6. a scale he calls horrific.

No date or time of day was given.

## Context: "bicycle bomb" was a standard term, not a rare one

- Malcolm Browne, AP's Saigon bureau chief until 1965, gives a whole passage to "the standard Viet Cong
  bicycle bomb", made by filling the frame tubing with explosive, and to a bicycle bomb "parked with
  hundreds of other bicycles." *The New Face of War* (1965; rev. ed. 1968), full-text search on the
  Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/newfaceofwar0000brow.
- The CIA/Interagency *Weekly Report: The Situation in South Vietnam*, 13 August 1964, reports that on
  12 August 1964 "a bomb planted in a parked bicycle" exploded at an outdoor restaurant in My Tho,
  killing 3 Vietnamese and wounding 5 US servicemen and 22 Vietnamese (MACV SITREP 222). Texas Tech
  Vietnam Center item 0410103001,
  https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/images.php?img=/images/041/0410103001.pdf. This was a month before his
  tour began and 40 miles from Saigon, so it is **not a candidate**.
- McGovern's insertion in the *Congressional Record*, 28 April 1965 (vol. 111, p. 8827), lists a
  bicycle bomb at the MAAG compound on 28 June 1963 and the My Tho bicycle bomb of 12 August 1964.
  https://archive.org/details/sim_congressional-record-proceedings-and-debates_april-28-1965-may-10-1965_111.

So a bystander in 1964–65 shouting "bicycle bomb" is ordinary. The words do not by themselves
point to one incident.

## Candidates, ranked by fit

### 1. The second charge at the Mỹ Cảnh, 25 June 1965 — a bicycle bomb among women and children, but not a market, and Americans were killed

**Confidence this is the Market story: plausible as the source of some details. Not established,
and not merged.**

- **Date and time:** Friday 25 June 1965, about 8:15 p.m.
- **Place:** the riverbank beside the floating restaurant on the Saigon River waterfront.
- **Mechanism:** two charges. AP's first-night story says one was "possibly an American-made
  Claymore electric mine — planted in the river bank", and "a second blast, which investigators
  believed was caused by a **bomb mounted on a bicycle**, went off at a tobacco stall on the bank
  next to the restaurant."
- **Victims on the street:** "Mothers out with the children were among the casualties." "Many of the
  injured were hit while strolling down the main waterfront boulevard." "A Vietnamese woman with
  blood on her face and dress squatted on the pavement cradling the bloody bodies of what appeared
  to be her two children."
- **Sources for the wire copy:** AP, Saigon, 25 June 1965, as printed in an unidentified US newspaper
  (Texas Tech item 1071004121, Glenn Helm Collection,
  https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/images.php?img=/images/107/1071004121.pdf); the same copy in the
  *Circleville Herald*, 25 June 1965, pp. 1–2 (https://archive.org/details/circleville-herald-1965-06-25);
  and "Bicycle Bomb … The other was a bicycle bomb" in the *Lincoln Star*, 26 June 1965
  (https://archive.org/details/lincoln-star-1965-06-26).
- **Later accounts:** Heather Stur, *Saigon at War* (Cambridge, 2020), ch. 1, puts the second bomb
  "attached to a bicycle that was leaning against a cigarette stand on the riverbank near the end of
  the gangway"
  (https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/saigon-at-war/heart-of-south-vietnam/006AF04EC09CAA74120898702F1F17DE).
  Rick Fredericksen, "The Attack that Shook the World," HistoryNet, 10 April 2017, has the sappers
  arriving by bicycle and one parking "his bicycle bomb" (https://historynet.com/the-attack-that-shook-the-world/).
  The accounts do not agree on which charge was on the bicycle.
- **Casualties:** 44 dead (27 Vietnamese, 13 U.S.) and 81 wounded (16 U.S.), per FRUS 1964–68 vol. III
  doc. 20, `sources/frus-1964-68-v03-d20`.

**Fits:** a bicycle bomb, which is the only contemporaneously reported one in Saigon during his tour
that this search found. Vietnamese mothers and children hurt on the street, not inside. Bar-going
hours. The waterfront where the bars of Tu Do end (the street geography is to be checked against a
1965 map).

**Does not fit:**

- "No Americans hurt": 13 U.S. dead and 16 U.S. wounded.
- It was not a market.
- His own Mỹ Cảnh account puts him at the door and entering the Peacock with Lopez, not running from
  "6 or 8 bars away" with 3 or 4 strangers.
- VD-0003 puts the M at the opposite end of the sheet from the river.
- "I didn't see any men there": the restaurant dead included many men.

**Why it is recorded anyway:** it is the only documented Saigon event of his tour that joins the
bicycle bomb and the women and children. The question of whether two memories have shared details
belongs here, in research. It must never be put to him as a question (rule 6).

### 2. Cholon, 20 August 1965 — a grenade in a market square, four wounded

**Confidence: poor fit. Keep as a documented market-square attack in his window, not as an
identification.**

- **UPI, as printed in the *San Rafael Daily Independent Journal*, 20 August 1965:** "In Saigon, a
  terrorist grenade went off in a crowded market square at 6:45 a.m. today, wounding four …
  exploded in the principal mar[ket] … the predominantly Chinese suburb of Cholon."
  https://archive.org/details/san-rafael-daily-independent-journal-1965-08-20. The OCR interleaves
  columns, so these are fragments pieced together, not a clean quotation.
- **AP, as printed in the *Appleton Post-Crescent*, 20 August 1965:** "Two terrorists on a motorbike
  threw a grenade and fired one shot today at a police station in Cholon, Saigon's Chinese section.
  Three police officers and a passerby were wounded. The terrorists escaped."
  https://archive.org/details/appleton-post-crescent-1965-08-20. This is also reconstructed from
  interleaved OCR. That it is the same incident as the UPI item is inferred from the date, the
  place and the count of four wounded. It is not stated in either.

**Fits:** a market square in greater Saigon, inside his tour (days before he left). Vietnamese
victims, and no Americans reported.

**Does not fit:**

- A grenade thrown from a motorbike, not a bicycle bomb.
- 6:45 a.m., not bar hours.
- The victims were police and one passerby, not women and children.
- Four wounded, which does not match "horrific".
- Cholon is not "6 or 8 bars" from Tu Do.

### 3. National Police (Special Branch) headquarters, Monday 16 August 1965

**Confidence: no fit.**

- **What happened:** explosive-laden jeep and car driven into the compound. Death toll 5 (all police)
  and 15 wounded, "possibly six Americans" among the hurt.
- **AP sources:** *Appleton Post-Crescent* 16 and 17 August 1965
  (https://archive.org/details/appleton-post-crescent-1965-08-16,
  https://archive.org/details/appleton-post-crescent-1965-08-17); *Circleville Herald* 16 August 1965.
- **AP context:** AP called it "the first serious terrorist incident in Saigon in seven weeks", that
  is, since the Mỹ Cảnh.
- **Date discrepancy:** the U.S. Mission's *Viet Cong Use of Terror: A Study* (Saigon, 1968) lists
  "August 18, 1965 — A Viet Cong bomb in Saigon kills six Vietnamese and wounds 15" (Texas Tech item
  2311312001, pp. 12–13, https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/images.php?img=/images/231/2311312001.pdf).
  Wikipedia's list gives "August 18: A bomb at the Police Directorate office in Saigon killed six
  and wounded 15". Its raw wikitext cites no source for the entry
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Viet_Cong_and_People%27s_Army_of_Vietnam_use_of_terror_in_the_Vietnam_War&action=raw).
  The 18th appears to be the 16th misdated. This is an inference from the matching toll and has
  not been verified.

**Does not fit:** not a market, vehicle bombs, the dead were police, and Americans were possibly
hurt.

### 4. The U.S. Embassy, 30 March 1965

**Confidence: no fit as the Market story. It is his, and already dated by him (`2026-09-03-phone-tu-do-explosion`).**

- **What happened:** a car bomb on Hàm Nghi Boulevard. The getaway driver was on a motorbike (AP,
  *Kingston Daily Freeman* 9 and 15 April 1965, on the trial of Nguyễn Văn Hai).
- **Casualties:** 2 Americans, 19 Vietnamese and 1 Filipino killed, 183 injured.

**Does not fit:** a car, not a bicycle; Americans killed; not a market.

The Embassy stood on the boulevard that, on modern maps, runs from the river toward the
central-market roundabout. That street geography is not yet checked against a 1965 map and
proves nothing about the M.

An AP map printed in the *Washington Post* on 5 December 1965 marks eight "Terrorist bombings in
Saigon in the past 18 months". They are the Metropole, Cộng Hòa Stadium (2 Oct 1965), National Police
HQ (16 Aug 1965), Mỹ Cảnh, U.S. Embassy, Brink Hotel, Caravelle Hotel (Aug 1964) and USNS *Card*
(May 1964). **None is a market** (Texas Tech item 1071007062,
https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/images.php?img=/images/107/1071007062.pdf). That map only picks out the
big incidents. A market bomb with no American victims could have gone unreported by the wire and
would still be missing from it.

## Context the search turned up: the central market in the 1965 news was an execution ground

This is context only, not a candidate. It is not for any rendered field, and it must not be put to
him.

- **The main market as an execution ground:** in the wire copy of his tour, the most-reported events
  at Saigon's main market were public executions by firing squad.
  - A 20-year-old Vietnamese was shot in "Saigon's main public market" an hour before sunrise
    (AP, *Appleton Post-Crescent* and *Circleville Herald* 29 Jan 1965).
  - A "sandbag execution parapet" was put up "in Saigon's main market place two hours after the
    airport explosion" (AP, *Appleton Post-Crescent* 16 Jun 1965).
  - Trần Văn Đang was executed "at dawn in Saigon's central market square while newsmen and
    photographers watched" (AP, *Sikeston Daily Standard*, *Appleton Post-Crescent* 22 Jun 1965).
    The PAVN account quoted by HistoryNet dates it 20 June and calls the Mỹ Cảnh its revenge.
- **Other reports:** AP's 25 June Mỹ Cảnh story also says "last year a bomb was set off across the
  street from the waterfront and several Vietnamese were killed". Its date is not found; it is
  likely before his tour, but that is not established.

## The VN-0120 first-aid scene: same event or a different one?

The question is open, and documents do not settle it either way. With no dated market explosion
found, there is nothing documentary to compare it with.

**His two accounts:**

- **VN-0120, 2026-09-03:** a bar "to the right of" the Palace, "a bomb of some kind", a man with a
  bleeding throat wrapped in his T-shirt, "nothing but wounded people all over the street."
- **The Market, 2026-09-12:** "6 or 8 bars away", a bicycle bomb, a woman whose bleeding he
  compressed until a Vietnamese took over, women and children, no men seen.

**Compared:**

- **Shared:** running to it, giving first aid, many wounded civilians in the street.
- **Different:** the landmark (Palace or Market), the casualty (a man's throat or a woman), what
  he did (a T-shirt or pressure by hand), and the weapon (unknown or a bicycle bomb).

The AP street description of the Mỹ Cảnh fits parts of both, and that is a reason for caution, not
a finding. Kept apart.
