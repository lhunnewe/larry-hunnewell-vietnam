# The "450 civilians" day — research plan

**Source:** Larry's Tell a Story comments, 2026-08-13
(`data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18006791.json`, `-c18006833.json`, `-c18006851.json`);
timeline entry `data/timeline/1965-08-450-civilians-accusation.json`.

**As told:** At the Special Forces camp on Núi Bà Đen (call sign **Body Jab 42**), on a day
when Sgt. Lowry was the only SF man present, a helicopter radioed Larry (his SAR call sign:
**Tay Ninh**) that hundreds of enemy were digging fighting positions outside the perimeter.
Larry relayed the warning to Lowry at the French fort. ~30 minutes later Lowry came running,
shouting "You son of a bitch! You killed 450 civilians down there," and ran off. Larry saw
nothing himself, radioed HQ at Tân Sơn Nhứt, and was extracted by **Rosethorn 007** within
about an hour. Days later he was diagnosed with jaundice and medevaced — placing the incident
in roughly late August 1965. He later heard a mass grave of 450 civilians was found at the
base of the mountain after the war. He wonders if he was made a scapegoat ("CIA or
something. I have no idea.").

**Sensitivity:** this has weighed on Larry for sixty years. Whatever research finds —
including "no incident documented" — goes to the family first, and Larry hears it gently or
not at all, at the family's judgment.

## Leads

1. **Call signs as unit identifiers.** "Body Jab 42" should identify the SF detachment
   (check 5th SF Group A-team designations and call-sign lists for Tây Ninh province,
   1965). "Rosethorn" should identify his parent aviation unit or command — check aviation
   call-sign registries and unit histories. Either would finally settle open question #2
   (the unit).
2. **The incident itself.** Search MACV / III Corps records, air-strike and artillery logs,
   and civilian-casualty investigations near Núi Bà Đen, ~July–September 1965. If hundreds
   of "enemy digging positions" were reported and fired on, a strike record should exist.
3. **The mass-grave account.** Larry heard 450 civilians were buried at the mountain's base,
   found after the war. Search post-war exhumation reports, Vietnamese provincial histories
   of Tây Ninh, and veteran accounts. Note: a well-documented mass grave *narrative* exists
   for other sites; treat any match cautiously and date it precisely.
4. **Sgt. Lowry** (open question #4) — the only named witness. Court-martial or
   investigation records, SF rosters, roster-rock names (VN-0035, VN-0133).
5. **Larry's own question.** He asked directly: "I would like to know if there were 450
   citizens killed." Answering him — either way, with evidence — is the goal.

---

## Findings — first research sweep, 2026-08-13

Four parallel web-research passes (English and Vietnamese sources), plus two declassified
CIA documents retrieved directly. Bottom lines:

### 1. No documentation of the incident was found — a genuine "not found"

No strike record, casualty investigation, Vietnamese commemoration, or civilian mass grave
at the mountain surfaced in any source searched, in either language. **This is not
disproof**: 1965 is the thinnest-documented American year in Tây Ninh (between the May 1964
SF seizure of the summit and the 1966 arrival of US divisions), and 1965 tactical-air and
artillery effects on villages were rarely investigated or publicly recorded. Vietnamese
local histories memorialize military "liệt sĩ" far more systematically than civilian dead.

### 2. A B-52 (Arc Light) strike is ruled out for the window

Retrieved the CIA's declassified Arc Light records covering exactly Larry's window
(Appendix I, "ARC LIGHT MISSIONS: 18 June 1965–15 November 1965,"
CIA-RDP78S02149R000100060004-0; Appendix IV, "Analysis of ARC LIGHT Program by Target
Zone" with the Figure 1/Figure 2 strike maps, CIA-RDP78S02149R000100060013-0; both
approved for release 2001-08-31, cia.gov/readingroom). The mission maps plot every strike
17 June–15 November 1965: **nothing near Núi Bà Đen in late July–early September 1965.**
August's 177 sorties fell in I Corps (BIRCH BARK, GEAR TRAIN, MAIN SPAR) and east of
Saigon (HOSE REEL, grid YT). The strikes actually near Tây Ninh city and the mountain
(CAT PAW I–VI, FINE SAND) came 28 October–14 November 1965 — **after Larry left**.
Whatever the helicopter's report triggered, it was not a B-52 mission: remaining candidate
mechanisms are tactical air (USAF/VNAF fighter-bombers), artillery, or armed helicopters —
none covered by these documents.

### 3. The "mass grave found after the war" detail most likely belongs to a different event

- The only well-documented mass civilian graves in Tây Ninh province are from the
  **25 September 1977 Khmer Rouge massacre** at Tân Lập and neighboring border communes
  (1,000+ killed, 506 in Tân Lập alone; memorialized) — wrong decade, wrong perpetrator,
  ~30–40 km from the mountain. A garbled secondhand version is a plausible source of the
  "450 buried at the mountain" story Larry heard decades later.
- Mass burials actually at/near the mountain that Vietnam memorializes are of **NLF
  soldiers, not civilians**: the 181-martyr stele at Chùa Hang on the mountainside
  (recon unit dead, associated with the Dec 1974–Jan 1975 fighting), and the Cầu Vịnh
  bomb-crater burial of "liệt sĩ" (~15–20 km away, excavation reported 2025).
- **Search-hazard warning:** everything online called "the Nui Ba Den Massacre" is the
  **13 May 1968** VC attack that killed 24 U.S. soldiers on the summit — not civilians,
  not 1965, not Larry's incident. And Ba Chúc (the famous 1978 Khmer Rouge massacre) is
  in An Giang province, not Tây Ninh. Family members googling should not mistake either
  for this event.
- One near-miss in a Vietnamese military-region newspaper series ("Núi Bà Đen những ngày
  khói lửa," baoquankhu7.vn): B-52s hit the Suối Núc forest in **October 1965**, killing
  3 district Party cadres — military/party dead, wrong month.

### 4. The setting checks out (see also research/units/callsigns-and-units.md)

The official Army history (Kelly, *U.S. Army Special Forces 1961–1971*, status tables)
lists **Núi Bà Đen itself as a CIDG camp in October 1964, manned by a half A-team — 1
officer, 5 enlisted — with ~175 CIDG strikers**; from January 1965 Detachment B-32 ran Tây
Ninh province. A skeleton SF crew, sometimes one man, matches Larry's account precisely.
"French Fort on the back side of Nui Ba Den" is attested verbatim as an American place
name (Nov 1967 photo caption). "Body Jab," "Tay Ninh," and "Rosethorn" appear in no public
call-sign source — consistent with monthly-rotated SOI voice call signs, which rarely
survive outside archival signal annexes.
