# Đồng Xoài: what Larry remembers, and what the paper printed at the time

**Written 2026-09-03 for the family, not for the archive's search log.** The working record of
how this was found is in `kia-misreport-stars-stripes.md`; this file is the plain version.

> **Do not show this to Larry, and do not read it to him.** It sets his account against a
> newspaper, and the whole method of this archive is that his memory goes down as he gives it
> and the differences live over here. If he is ever asked about Đồng Xoài again, he should be
> asked cold, with none of what follows supplied. See CLAUDE.md, rules 1 and 6.

---

## What he told us

On 1 September 2026, asked nothing more than *what the camp on the mountain was for*, he
answered the question and then kept going, into something nobody had asked about:

> They (the people at the camp at Dong Xoai, special forces team, there were about 16 people,
> they were attacked by about 4000 enemy all at once at 4am in the morning, I was sitting at
> the radio when it happened, guy on radio was dying from shot in the stomach, Gagne was with
> me and died, he was a french foreign Legionair) could get us on the radio and were like 40
> miles away…

The following day he added why, as he understood it, nobody came to help:

> There was no aircraft up to help the guys at Dong Xoai at all, everything was grounded.
> That's why the Vietcong attacked because they knew none of the helicopters could fly.

So: a pre-dawn attack, roughly sixteen defenders, about four thousand attackers, a man dying on
the other end of the radio, and no air support because the weather had grounded everything.

---

## What the paper printed, four days later

**Pacific Stars and Stripes, Saturday 12 June 1965, front page.** The headline is
**"VIET BATTLE RAGES"**, above a kicker reading **"31 American Casualties"**, over a wire photo
of smoke pouring off the buildings at Đồng Xoài.

The story, in substance:

- An estimated **1,500 to 2,000** guerrillas struck the town in a **pre-dawn assault on
  Thursday** (10 June).
- They overwhelmed a defending force of **American Army Seabees, Special Forces personnel and
  government troops**.
- American casualties stood at **31** — 13 wounded, and 18 either killed or missing.
- **The missing included eight Americans from two helicopters shot down by enemy fire.**
- **The first government relief force sent in by helicopter was "torn apart by heavy enemy
  fire."**
- Vietnamese rangers who stormed the compound lost 30 killed and 15 wounded.

And the next day's paper, 13 June, carried a continuation that mentions the **118th Aviation
Company** flying **"through heavy Viet Cong fire to evacuate the wounded."** That is the unit
this archive believes Larry was attached to or worked for.

## Putting the two side by side

| | Larry | The paper |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | "4am in the morning" | "a **pre-dawn** assault Thursday" |
| **How many attackers** | "about 4000" | "an estimated 1,500 to 2,000" |
| **How many defenders** | "about 16 people, special forces team" | Seabees, Special Forces and government troops |
| **Air support** | "no aircraft up to help… everything was grounded" | two helicopters **shot down**; the relief force flown in and "torn apart by heavy enemy fire" |

### The timing is right, and it is remarkable that it is

He gave the hour cold — no one mentioned Đồng Xoài to him, no one showed him anything, and he
was answering a question about radio physics. **Sixty-one years later he put the attack in the
pre-dawn dark, and the contemporary wire report says pre-dawn.** That is the cleanest match
between his testimony and a document that this archive has ever produced.

### The numbers are about double

Four thousand against fifteen hundred to two thousand. Not a wild invention — the right order
of magnitude, and the sort of figure that swells a little every time a story is told. It is
recorded as a difference. **His words are not edited.**

### The air support is where his account and the record part

This is the one that matters, and it repays reading slowly.

He says nothing could fly. The paper says aircraft flew, two were shot down with eight men
aboard missing, and a relief force went in by helicopter and was destroyed trying.

**But notice what he got right inside the thing he got wrong.** What he remembers is that
**help did not get through** — and it did not. Men went in and were killed doing it. What is
wrong is not the outcome. It is the **reason** he gives for the outcome: he remembers weather,
the record says enemy fire.

That is one of the most ordinary things memory does. The outcome is what you live through and
keep. The explanation is assembled afterwards, out of whatever makes sense of it, and then it
hardens and gets told alongside the part you actually witnessed, in the same voice, with the
same confidence.

## Why this makes his testimony more useful, not less

It is tempting to read all this as "his memory is unreliable." That is the wrong lesson and it
would be unfair to him.

What it actually gives us is a **shape** — a way of knowing which parts of anything he says can
be leaned on:

- **Times, sequences and outcomes: strong.** The 4 a.m. is exact. He has been right before on
  this kind of detail — the mission of the mountain camp, which matched an official document he
  had never seen; the Uppercut call sign, which this archive had predicted in writing before he
  said it.
- **Counts and scales: inflated.** Four thousand for two thousand. Expect roughly double, and
  never build on his figures alone.
- **Causes and explanations: reconstructions.** Not lies, not confusion — the ordinary human
  work of making a remembered event make sense. **They must never be treated as things he saw.**

### Where that applies right now

Two open questions in this archive turn on causal claims of exactly this kind.

**The "450 civilians" day.** His defence is a causal claim: that he could not have called in a
strike because he never changed his radio station and had no way to reach aircraft. That is
precisely the category this finding says to test rather than accept — not because he is lying,
but because it is the kind of statement his memory constructs. The good news is that it is
testable against doctrine and the net structure, and if it holds it will hold much harder for
having been checked.

**The KIA report.** He says "probably Sgt Lowery, probably reported me KIA" — and he flagged it
as inference himself, twice, in one sentence. He is a better witness about his own uncertainty
than we sometimes are on his behalf.

## What is still not known

- Where he was sitting when Đồng Xoài was hit — the mountain, or Saigon. He said the mountain on
  2026-09-02.
- **Gagne.** He said Gagne died there; the next day he said Gagne did not die and was alive when
  Larry was flown out. Both accounts stand.
- The man dying of a stomach wound on the other radio. A period memoir has that camp's radio
  officer rescued alive. Not reconciled.
