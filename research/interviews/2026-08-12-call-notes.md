# Call notes — August 12, 2026 (phone call with Larry)

**Research note, 2026-08-12.** Notes relayed from a morning phone call between Larry and his
son, written down the same day. These are the son's notes of Larry's words, not a verbatim
transcript — fidelity is **paraphrase**. Recorded as given; discrepancies with other evidence
go in the analysis section below, never back into his account.

## What Larry said

- **A sergeant's name** — believed to be **"Chapman"** or something like that. *(Context not
  captured in the notes — which sergeant this refers to is an open clarification, see below.)*
- **Japan — the friend in the photos.** The man in the Japan pictures was **in the bed across
  from Larry (or next to him)**, and they hung out from that day. He was **from someplace back
  east**. They adventured around for a day or so in Japan: went to **the Ginza strip** and
  found a sign that said **"Heidelberg"** [notes: "Heidalburg"] — a **German restaurant that
  was downstairs** — and went down and ate there.
- **Why he was in Japan: he was sick.** He went to Japan because he was ill with
  **"sepititis"** [as noted — likely his word for **hepatitis**, see analysis], **malaria, and
  infections**, and **lost a bunch of weight**. He described how sick he was: **his stool was
  pure white and his urine looked like Coca-Cola**.
- **Weather in Japan** was cold — "it was going into winter." He placed it as **early
  September**.
- **Kennedy.** He was **about a month into basic training when Kennedy was shot**.

Separate note from the son, same day: **not certain the man at the beach table (VN-0022) is
the same man in the Japan photos (VN-0093, VN-0095)** — they may be two different people.
The hospital-bed detail above supports that possibility: if Larry met the Japan companion in
the ward, he could be a hospital friend, not the Vietnam friend.

## Two more story fragments (added same day)

The call was short, but Larry briefly touched two more stories:

1. **The helicopter and the explosion.** Something about a helicopter, an explosion, and two
   people — the son's impression was **two females or girls** — **with bombs around them**.
   Larry referred to them with a word the son heard phonetically as **"su-ners"** ("sooners,"
   "sunners," or something like that). *Note from the son: "I may have assumed they were
   female" — the gender may be an inference, but the word was Larry's.*
2. **The bathroom knife story (not from this call).** Larry has told, in past conversations,
   of **almost being stabbed in a bathroom by another military person**. He did not retell it
   today; the fragment is recorded here so it isn't lost. To be captured in his own words
   when he brings it up.

**On "su-ners":** the leading hypothesis is **"sappers"** — the standard term for Viet Cong
demolition commandos who infiltrated installations with explosive charges strapped to their
bodies; female VC sappers are documented. The phonetic is preserved exactly as heard and the
hypothesis stays out of his record until he retells the story. If confirmed, "sapper" joins
the glossary and the story may match a documentable attack.

## Analysis (research, not recollection)

1. **The Kennedy memory independently corroborates the DD-214 draft date.** Kennedy was shot
   **November 22, 1963**. "About a month into basic training" counted from induction on
   **October 28, 1963** (DD-214, item 18) is a match within days. Larry's own memory now
   supports the 1963 induction over the old family estimate of 1964 — from his own mouth,
   without being prompted by the form.
2. **The symptoms he described are textbook hepatitis.** Pale/white ("clay-colored") stool
   plus dark, cola-colored urine are the classic presentation of jaundice from hepatitis —
   which was widespread among U.S. troops in Vietnam. "Sepititis" as noted is almost
   certainly his (or the note-taker's) rendering of **hepatitis** — though sepsis from the
   "infections" isn't ruled out. **This likely answers unresolved question #7**: the USARJ
   medical command on the DD-214 was a **medical evacuation as a patient**, not a
   reassignment. (Consistent with box 27 "NONE" — illness, not combat wounds.)
3. **The hospital-bed friend reframes the Japan photo series.** If the companion in VN-0093
   was the man in the next bed, the "recurring dark-haired friend" thread in the interview
   guide (question #2) may be **two different men**: a Vietnam friend (beach, guitar, pool)
   and a Japan hospital friend "from back east." Do not merge them without Larry looking at
   both photos side by side.
4. **Date tension to resolve gently.** "Early September" and "cold, going into winter" don't
   fit together in Tokyo (early September is warm). His foreign service ran to separation on
   November 30, 1965, so a Japan stay of roughly **September–November 1965** would cover both:
   arrival in early September *and* genuinely cold weather by the end. The Ginza outing may
   simply be later in the stay than the arrival. Also note: the KIA-report sighting is
   currently dated "about August 1965" *in Japan* — if he reached Japan in early September,
   the sighting window may shift to September or later. Ask, don't assume.
5. **The Heidelberg restaurant is a findable anchor.** A basement German restaurant called
   "Heidelberg" on or near the Ginza in 1965 should be traceable through Tokyo directories,
   English-language guidebooks for servicemen, or Stars and Stripes ads — and would put a
   verified pin in the Japan chapter.
6. **"Sergeant Chapman" needs a referent** before it can join the people files — candidates
   include the Núi Bà Đen sergeant currently carried as "Lowry/Lowery," a basic-training
   sergeant, a unit sergeant, or someone from the Japan period.

## Clarifications (same day, from the son)

Answers to the analysis questions above, given 2026-08-12:

  from Vietnam. He did not mention a hospital name; that's a question to ask.

  real, separate names. The Chapman comment came up **around the basic-training part of the
  conversation** — so working hypothesis: a basic-training-era sergeant.
- **"Early September" was when he was in Japan for the cold weather** (i.e., his arrival);
  the Ginza day was a day he and the friend he met there explored Japan together. **He does
  not remember the friend's name.**
- **The beach-table man and the Japan man are treated as different people** until Larry says
  otherwise. Next step: get Larry to tell a story about the beach person.
- **The KIA newspaper**: it came up — he saw it in Japan, but where in Japan is unclear.
  Question queued: *"Did you see it in the hospital ward?"*
