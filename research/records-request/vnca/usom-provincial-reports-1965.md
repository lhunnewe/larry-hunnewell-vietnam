## What these documents are, and why they carry weight

Unclassified monthly summaries of the reports filed by the USOM (later USAID) civilian
provincial representative in each province, compiled by the Reports Officer, MRCC
(Provincial Operations), Saigon, and distributed to the Ambassador's office, Lansdale's
Mission Group, JUSPAO, and the USOM division chiefs. One or two pages per province, north
to south, every month. The provincial rep was the resident American civilian whose job
included exactly the things a mass civilian-casualty event produces: refugees, relocation
allowances, damage claims, public reaction.

Crucially, **these reports do record American mistakes, by name and date.** From the same
months, other provinces:

- **Kiến Hòa, August 1965:** "On 16 August US planes mistakenly destroyed a Catholic Church
  and school, the Buddhist temple and the Cao-Dai Pagoda, all of which were not intended to
  be hit. All were in the same village and a number of civilians were killed."
- **Vĩnh Long, August 1965:** nightly 105s and near-daily air strikes, "and as always, these
  strikes had their occasional tragic aftermath: civilian population killed and wounded" —
  with the province's gun tubes condemned as "not reliably accurate at ranges exceeding one
  mile," and a backlog of over 200 unpaid indemnification claims, "66 have been death claims
  and two thirds of those deaths were caused by friendly air or artillery."
- **Quảng Ngãi / Quảng Nam, August 1965:** a demonstration of "an estimated one thousand
  people" against air strikes; "Sixteen persons were wounded, and two were killed during the
  dispersal of crowds."
- **Hậu Nghĩa, July 1965** (the province bordering Tây Ninh): two civilian demonstrations
  against artillery firing — 25–30 women at Bảo Trai on 15 July, and **400 people (mostly
  women) at Trưng Lập on 26 July**. The rep is scathing about unobserved and interdictory
  fire: the province "even joke about how useless they are," and he "is convinced that this
  type action is alienating the civilian population beyond all hope of recall."
- **Hậu Nghĩa, August 1965:** a demonstration on 30 July "involved an estimated **460
  women**," and a loudspeaker helicopter told Cu Chi district that areas were now "free
  bombing zones."
- **November 1965, headquarters overview:** "The problem of misdirected or erroneous
  bombardments by friendly forces continues to rear its head from time to time," with
  Bình Định and Quảng Trị named, and a standing Ministry of Social Welfare process for
  assisting "victims of erroneous bombardments by friendly forces."

So the channel was live, candid, and specific in exactly Larry's window.

## What it says about Tây Ninh — the negative

**July 1965 (Tây Ninh 24):** the change of province chief from Gen. Lê Văn Tất to Maj. Hồ
Đức Trung and the Cao Đài boycott; "a marked increase in VC activity"; company-strength
attacks on RF/PF outposts; a 20 July grenade attack on Tây Ninh City wounding three RF
soldiers; four doctors for a province of 232,000. No civilian-casualty event.

**August 1965 (Tây Ninh 24):** "no marked change in the political situation"; "There is no
significant change in the security situation"; the Special Forces' pacification effort
"beginning to reach further into areas on which the Viet Cong hold was formerly very
strong." **No incident of any kind involving civilian deaths.** This is the month the
timeline places the accusation.

**November 1965 (Tây Ninh 24):** "The province was relatively quiet during the month and
except for a few minor actions involving few troops the province never enjoyed a more secure
period." (Also a period detail worth keeping: the province could not spend its road budget
"if crushed rock from Nui Ba Den cannot be secured.")

**Assessment.** For July, August, and November 1965 the American civilian on the ground in
Tây Ninh reported nothing resembling 450 civilian dead, in a reporting series that named
such events elsewhere in the same months and had a live compensation process attached. That
is a real negative and the strongest one yet recorded against the literal figure. Its limits,
stated plainly: these are **unclassified summaries**, one page a month, compiled for
distribution; a classified or military-channel event need not appear; the provincial rep
lived in Tây Ninh City, not on the mountain; and "no report" is not "no event."

## What it says about Tây Ninh — the September document

The Region III report for the month ending **30 September 1965** is the closest thing to a
contemporary account of the mountain that this project has found. Its Tây Ninh section, in
the rep's own words:

> "The military picture in Tay Ninh province did not appear to have changed perceptibly.
> What activity there was seemed to be confined to **air strikes and artillery barrages**,
> with comparatively little ground action. **North of the mountain Nui Ba Den, an operation
> was launched to burn down a VC village and escort the local population to sectors under
> our control. This was done and the population resettled. The next day, however, they were
> gone — back to the burned-down village beyond the mountain.**"

He goes on to describe aircraft defoliating "large areas under VC control," his doubt that
it would drive anyone out, and closes: "free bombing, massed artillery fire and defoliation
are techniques which, whatever else they may accomplish, are hardly likely to win over the
population" — with an explicit note that this is "the personal opinion of one individual
deeply committed to what the US is doing here."

What this does and does not establish:

- **It does establish** that in Larry's last weeks in country, at Núi Bà Đen, there was an
  operation against a village at the mountain, that air strikes and artillery were the
  province's main military activity that month, and that the population of that village was
  moved rather than killed — and walked home the next day.
- **It does not establish** that this was the day Larry remembers, and it is not a record of
  the event he was accused of causing. The report is a monthly summary with no dates
  attached to the operation, and the accusation as he tells it involved deaths, not a
  relocation.
- **It is the first period document** that puts an American-directed operation against
  civilians' homes at Núi Bà Đen inside the window — the setting of the accusation, without
  its casualties.

None of this is proof either way, and it must not be presented to Larry as an answer. What
it changes is the shape of the question: from "did a mass killing at the mountain go
unrecorded?" to "what happened to the village north of the mountain, and is the operation
the rep describes the same event the sergeant shouted about?"

## New record trail found in these documents

The Vĩnh Long report names the mechanism by which a civilian death caused by friendly air or
artillery generated paper in 1965: **MIDCAP / MILCAP indemnification claims, docketed by
Sector S-5** and settled by a provincial indemnification committee. If civilians were killed
near Núi Bà Đen, Tây Ninh Sector's S-5 docket is where a claim would sit. Add to the NARA
RG 472 request: **Tây Ninh Sector (Advisory Team 90 / Det B-32 area) S-5 records and MIDCAP
indemnification claim files, 1965.**

# Phuoc Long — the province Đồng Xoài sits in — 2026-09-03

**No request was needed.** These reports are free and downloadable from the open VVA portal, and
**the July 1965 report already cited by this archive turns out to contain a Phuoc Long section
that nobody had read.** It was mined for Tây Ninh in September and the rest of the document was
left.

## A TRAP THAT PRODUCED A FALSE NEGATIVE — read this before searching any VVA scan

The first greps of the July PDF returned **zero** hits for "Phuoc Long", "Tay Ninh", *and even
"Province"* — in a document titled *Reports of USOM Provincial Representatives*. That was not
absence. **The OCR text layer has every character doubled**, e.g. "RREEPOORRTTSS" for "REPORTS"
and "PPrroovviinncciiaall" for "Provincial".

Plain grep finds nothing, and a careless session would record a confident false negative.
**Search these scans with a doubling-tolerant pattern** — a regex allowing each character to
repeat, case-insensitive. With that applied the same document yields Phuoc Long x3, Tay Ninh x3,
Dong Xoai x1, Province x174.

*The doubling is not perfectly uniform, so do not simply take every other character.*

## What the Phuoc Long report says — month ending 31 July 1965

Written by the resident American civilian representative, about six weeks after Đồng Xoài:

> The fact remains that **Song-Be, along with three other district areas were either overrun or
> hit very bad**, despite the presence and efforts of GVN. As people anywhere, they fear for
> their lives... **Nearly every night one or more of the district headquarters are probed.**
>
> Economically, the province is in bad shape. **All bridges out of the capital to the districts
> have been blown up during the past two weeks.** All of our supplies have to be air-lifted in...
> The people are worried about **another attack on Song Be**, since the VC have announced they
> would try again.

Đồng Xoài is not named — it is one of the "three other district areas" — and the rest of the
report is cement, roofing nails for the artillery battery headquarters, rice, fuel, and a planned
sewing centre.

**What matters is what is absent. There is no civilian casualty figure, no massacre, no mass
grave, no body count of any kind** — in the report of the American civilian official resident in
the province where the battle happened, six weeks after it. That is exactly the texture the Tây
Ninh reports showed: **these documents do not enumerate the dead.** It confirms, from a second
province, that the silence of this series is a property of the series and not evidence about any
particular event.

## The one Đồng Xoài mention — and it is the civilian aftermath

It appears not under Phuoc Long but under **Binh Duong**, in the refugee accounting at Phu Cuong:

> Of the 2400 odd refugees in Phu Cuong, a large percentage have been dispersed to other areas;
> to Gia Dinh for resettlement, **back home to Dong Xoai**, and one group at Lai Thieu is still
> supported by Social Welfare. At present there is a total of **771 families of refugees
> consisting of 3,476 persons — 1,346 adults and 2,131 children.**

So the people who fled Đồng Xoài were counted, housed and in part sent home, and the record of it
sits in a neighbouring province's report. **Displacement is recorded in this series where death
is not** — the same pattern as the September Region III report on the village burned north of
Núi Bà Đen, whose population was *moved rather than killed*.
