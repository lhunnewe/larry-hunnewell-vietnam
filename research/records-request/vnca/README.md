## The headline: VNCA is not where these records are

The reply is a reference-and-guidance answer rather than a holdings answer, and it redirects
the core of the request:

> "All preserved military and government records from the Vietnam era are at the U.S.
> National Archives and Records Administration (NARA II, College Park, Maryland)."

VNCA **collects only by donation** — from veterans and their families, and from researchers
who compiled material on a topic. Over 3,600 donors. Consequences, in their words:

- **No medical unit records**; "very few unit rosters or records at the smaller unit level."
- Personal service or medical records only if that individual donated them.
- They "do not compile era statistics or provide historical analysis."

So items 1–3 of our request (MACV J-3 / III Corps journals, 5th SFG and Det B-32 operational
summaries, civilian-casualty investigations) are **NARA RG 472 requests**, not TTU requests.
What TTU can still hold is the donated shadow of those records: a veteran's own copy of a
journal page, a compiler's research file, photographs, maps, oral histories. That is worth
searching, and it is what the rest of the reply is about.

## Their NARA advice (worth following exactly)

- NARA **does no research for you.** They "pull and scan the records you specifically
  request," so a request must name **unit, date(s), and record type(s)** precisely.
- Hundreds of requests a week; expect a backlog.
- Start with NARA's "Ask the Archives" for their own recommendations.
- NARA can supply a **list of independent researchers for hire** experienced at College
  Park, who will look for relevant material rather than scanning pages we guessed at. For a
  question this narrow — one province, one three-month window, a named detachment — a paid
  onsite researcher may be the shortest path. A family decision, and a cost.
- Caveat the archivist attached to all of it: "I've never researched at NARA so can only
  share what I've found."

**Scope caveat — do not over-read the zeros.** These search *item records*. Only about 20%
of VNCA holdings are digitized; the rest is discoverable **only through collection Finding
Aids**. A 0 in the item database means "nothing digitized and described with these terms,"
not "the archive holds nothing." The Finding Aid sweep is a separate search, still unrun.

Also from their search tips, bearing directly on the zeros: the archive has **no controlled
vocabulary** — items are described in the words found in each item — and spacing changes
results (`Da Nang` ≠ `Danang`). Retries worth running: `B-32`, `B 32`, `Tay Ninh` alone,
`Nui Ba Den` / `Black Virgin Mountain`, `CIDG`, `5th Special Forces`, `SOI`, `CEOI`,
`signal operating instructions` — and a date-span-free version of each, because items
catalogued "No Date" never retrieve when a date span is set.

## How to work their database (from the reply and the attached guides)

- **Two portals, same interface and results:**
  - <https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu> — open.
  - <https://readingroom.vietnam.ttu.edu/> — registration and login, and it **unlocks
    copyright-blocked digitized items** for personal and research use (click the copyright
    icon in the item record). Register: it is strictly more than the open portal.
- **Advanced Search** is recommended; the box is a database field, not a sentence — a full
  sentence searches on every word in it.
- **Permalinks are the citation unit.** Search-result URLs expire. The permanent form always
  ends in the item number:
  `https://www.vietnam.ttu.edu/virtualarchive/items.php?item=13680101001`. Downloaded files
  are named by item number. **Any VNCA item we cite becomes a `data/sources/` record keyed
  to its item number and permalink.**
- **Filters** (left of the results) include an "Available Online?" filter to drop
  copyrighted and undigitized items, and a **Thesaurus** filter that surfaces additional era
  terms — the intended cure for the no-controlled-vocabulary problem.
- **Finding Aids** are the only route to the undigitized 80%. A collection's Finding Aid item
  number is its 3–4 digit collection number plus seven zeros (collection 2397 →
  `23970000000`). Inside a Finding Aid, a live link means digitized; "No Records" means the
  material exists but is not scanned.
- **Ordering scans of undigitized material:** email VNCA with **Collection Name & Number,
  Box#, Folder#**, and the document title if only one document from a folder is wanted. Scans
  are for private use and research, quotable with credit, not reproducible without VNCA and,
  where applicable, the copyright holder. Each item record carries a VNCA credit line and a
  Chicago-style citation behind a "Citation" button.
- Posted files are low resolution; **high-resolution image, audio, and film files are
  available on request**.

## Maps — the most immediately usable offer

- VNCA has the era **U.S. Army 1:50,000 Topographic L7014 series** free online, including
  high-resolution PDFs to view, download, or print.
- They also hold a nearly complete **1:25,000 Pictomap L8020 supplement series (~1968)** —
  two sheets per 1:50,000 sheet, designated by map sheet number plus **N** or **S**. **Not
  online.** Email VNCA the sheet numbers and they send the PDFs.
- Veteran-compiled **Index of Fire Support Bases and Landing Zones … with Map Sheet Numbers
  and Coordinates** (William Kindred):
  <https://www.vietnam.ttu.edu/virtualarchive/items.php?item=25720206001>

A cheap, concrete next action: find the L7014 sheet covering Núi Bà Đen and Tây Ninh city,
then request its two Pictomap halves. A 1:25,000 pictorial sheet of the mountain is the best
chance of putting names to the settlements "down there" below the camp — the ones the
accusation refers to — and of locating the **French fort** on the back side of the mountain
that Larry describes.
