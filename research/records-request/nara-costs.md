# What a NARA request actually costs — checked 2026-09-01

Written because the question was asked before the request was drafted, which is the right
order. Figures are from NARA's own published fee schedule,
<https://www.archives.gov/research/order/fees>, read 2026-09-01.

## Free

- **Asking is free.** Reference enquiries — "do you hold this, where is it, how would I
  request it" — cost nothing. NARA's "Ask the Archives" service is the front door, and
  Texas Tech's reference desk specifically recommended starting there.
- **The National Archives Catalog is free** to search, and a growing part of Record Group
  472 is digitized in it. Anything already scanned can be downloaded at no cost. **This
  should be exhausted before a single page is ordered.**
- **Visiting in person is free**, and researchers may photograph records with their own
  camera at no charge. For a large pull, a day at College Park with a phone camera costs
  travel and nothing else.

## What NARA charges if they do the work

| Service | Fee |
|---|---|
| Paper copy, up to 11×17 | **$0.80 per page** |
| Basic digitised scan, up to 8½×14 | **$0.80 per scan** |
| Oversized basic scan | $3.50 per scan |
| Enhanced digitised scan | $20.00 per scan |
| Colour paper copy | $4.60 per page |
| **Minimum order** | **$20.00** (covers 25 pages or fewer at the standard rate) |
| Self-service copying or scanning on site | $0.25 each |
| Expedited shipping | $30.00 per order, or actual cost if higher |

**So the shape of the cost is:** a $20 minimum, then about **80 cents a page**. A hundred
pages is roughly $80. A monthly operational report of 200 pages is roughly $160.

This is why the precision of the request matters so much, and why Texas Tech's advice was
worth following exactly. NARA pulls and scans **what you name** — they do no research. A
vague request produces either nothing or a large bill for pages nobody wanted. A request
naming **Detachment A-324, Núi Bà Đen, Tây Ninh, 1964–65**, with A-325A and A-353 as
alternates, and naming the record types, is both likelier to succeed and cheaper.

## The paid alternative, with no number attached

NARA will supply a **list of independent researchers for hire** who work at College Park.
They are private contractors: **NARA does not set or publish their rates**, so any figure
quoted here would be invented. The honest position is that this route needs **written quotes
before anyone commits**, exactly as the film request to Texas Tech does.

What it buys is judgement. A researcher can look at a finding aid, pull the right box, and
photograph twenty relevant pages instead of scanning two hundred guessed ones. For a question
this narrow — one province, one detachment, a three-month window — that can cost less than
ordering blind, not more.

## Recommended order of spending

1. **Ask the Archives** — free. Confirm what exists and where before ordering anything.
2. **Search the National Archives Catalog** — free. Take whatever is already digitised.
3. **Order the specific documents named in the request** — $20 minimum, ~$0.80/page. Start
   with the single highest-value item rather than everything at once.
4. **Only then** consider an independent researcher, with a written quote, if steps 1–3 show
   the material exists but is too scattered to order blind.

**Nothing above step 3 needs a decision today.** The draft itself costs nothing, and steps 1
and 2 are free.
