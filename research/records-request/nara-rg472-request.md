**Subject: Reference request — RG 472, 5th Special Forces Group and III Corps records, Tây Ninh Province, 1964–1965**

Dear Textual Reference staff,

I am researching my father's U.S. Army service in Vietnam, September 1964 to September 1965.
He was a radio operator whose headquarters was at Tân Sơn Nhứt and who was detached for
periods to the Special Forces camp on **Núi Bà Đen (Black Virgin Mountain), Tây Ninh
Province**. He is 86, and we are assembling a documented record of his service while we can
still ask him questions.

I would be grateful for your advice on what the following record sets contain, where they
sit in Record Group 472, and what reproduction would cost. I am asking first rather than
ordering, so that I request the right boxes rather than guess.

The camp is identified in compiled references as **Detachment A-324, Núi Bà Đen, Tây Ninh
Province / Phú Khương District, grid XT281582, opened July 1964, mission listed as radio
relay**, under **Detachment B-32 (Tây Ninh)** and **Company C / C-3, III Corps**. Two other
detachment designations are associated with the same site and may be how records are filed:
**A-325A** and **A-353**.

**1. 5th Special Forces Group (Airborne), 1st Special Forces — Operational Reports /
Lessons Learned, all reporting periods in calendar year 1965.** I have seen the report for
the period ending 31 December 1967, whose annexes tabulate every detachment with its
commander, camp, province, grid coordinate and code word, and which narrates operations from
individual camps. The 1965 equivalents are the single documents most likely to answer my
questions, and I would like these first if I can have only one thing.

**2. Records of Detachment A-324 (alternatively A-325A or A-353), Núi Bà Đen, and of
Detachment B-32, Tây Ninh Province, July 1964 – December 1965** — monthly operational
summaries, after-action reports, duty rosters, or any detachment-level file.

**3. Signal Operating Instructions (SOI) or CEOI, and any signal annexes, for III Corps
and/or the 5th Special Forces Group, 1964–1965.** My father remembers the voice call signs
in use around him as boxing terms — his camp was "Body Jab 42," with "Right Cross" and
"Upper Cut" on the same net, and he recalls "Rosethorn 007" as the aircraft that extracted
him. A signal annex naming any of these would confirm both the scheme and the units. I
understand SOI documents were changed frequently and often destroyed; if none survive for
1965, knowing that is itself useful.

**4. MACV J-3 and/or III Corps daily staff journals and operational summaries, July–September
1965**, particularly any entries concerning Núi Bà Đen, its camp, or air or artillery
strikes near the mountain.

**5. Tây Ninh Sector (Province) advisory records for 1965 — specifically S-5 files and
MIDCAP/MILCAP indemnification claim files.** Period USOM provincial reports describe this as
the channel through which civilian casualties caused by friendly air or artillery generated
claims, docketed by Sector S-5. I am trying to establish whether any such claims were filed
in Tây Ninh Province in 1965.

**6. After-action reports for two Special Forces camp attacks my father describes hearing
by radio:** **Đồng Xoài, 9–10 June 1965**, and **Bu Dop, 20 July 1965**.

If any of these are held elsewhere — I understand Army morning reports for this period are
at the National Personnel Records Center rather than College Park — I would be glad to be
told so, and to be pointed at the right place.

I am glad to pay reproduction fees. **Could you give me an estimate before any work is done
if it would exceed roughly $50**, so I can prioritise. I would also welcome your list of
independent researchers who work at College Park, which the Vietnam Center and Archive at
Texas Tech suggested I ask for.

Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,
[Your name]

## Notes before sending

- **Do the catalog search first.** It is free and it may remove one or two items from the
  list entirely.
- **Item 1 is the one to protect.** If the reply offers only one thing, take the 1965 5th SFG
  operational reports. They would carry the detachment's commander and code word for Larry's
  year and are the most likely single source of a "Body Jab" confirmation.
- **The $50 estimate threshold** is a suggestion. NARA's minimum order is $20 and copies run
  about $0.80 a page (`nara-costs.md`), so $50 is roughly the point at which a request stops
  being trivial. Adjust it to whatever the family is comfortable with.
- **Item 5 is the sensitive one.** It is worded as a records question and deliberately does
  not tell the story behind it. Anything it returns goes to the family first
  (`../events/450-civilians-nui-ba-den.md`).
- **Not asked for here, on purpose:** the aviation unit question. Larry's own unit — the
  "118th AOD," the 145th Aviation Battalion, the UTT/68th, and his statement that he "really
  worked for 1st Cavalry" — is a different search with different record series, and folding
  it into this letter would blur a request whose strength is its precision. It deserves its
  own enquiry once the NPRC file names his unit. Ledger #2, issues #10 and #57.
- **His age is 86.** The Texas Tech letter of 2026-08-13 said 82, which was wrong; do not
  copy the number forward from that draft.
