**Subject: Reference request — MACV/5th SF Group records, Núi Bà Đen (Tây Ninh), July–September 1965**

Dear Vietnam Center & Sam Johnson Vietnam Archive staff,

I am researching the 1964–65 Vietnam service of my father, a U.S. Army radio operator who
was detached for periods to the Special Forces CIDG camp at Núi Bà Đen (Black Virgin
Mountain), Tây Ninh province. He is 82 and helping us reconstruct his service; one event
from the end of his tour has weighed on him for sixty years, and we are trying to find any
contemporary record of it.

The event, as he remembers it: on a day in roughly **late July–early September 1965**, he
relayed a helicopter pilot's radio report of "hundreds of enemy digging fighting positions"
outside the camp perimeter at Núi Bà Đen. About half an hour later, the Special Forces
sergeant at the camp accused him of having caused the deaths of ~450 civilians "down
there." My father never saw or learned what actually happened; he was medically evacuated
to Japan days later. We have already ruled out a B-52 Arc Light strike using the
declassified CIA mission maps for June–November 1965, so if anything happened it would
have been tactical air, artillery, or armed helicopters.

Could you advise whether the archive holds, and how we might obtain copies from, the
following record sets for **Tây Ninh province, July–September 1965**:

1. **MACV J-3 or III Corps daily journals / operational summaries** for that window,
   particularly any entries mentioning Núi Bà Đen, its CIDG camp, or air/artillery strikes
   near the mountain;
2. **5th Special Forces Group records** — monthly operational summaries or after-action
   reports of **Detachment B-32** (Tây Ninh province) and of the CIDG camp at Núi Bà Đen
   (which the Kelly official history lists as a camp with a half-size A-team in October
   1964);
3. Any **civilian casualty reports or investigations** for Tây Ninh province in that
   window;
4. As a secondary matter, any **Signal Operating Instructions / call-sign documents** for
   III Corps units in 1965 — we are trying to identify the units behind the voice call
   signs he remembers ("Body Jab," "Rosethorn," "Tay Ninh").

We would be glad to pay reproduction or research fees, and we can be precise about dates
if that helps narrow a search. Any guidance — including "those records are at NARA, not
here" — would be genuinely appreciated.

Thank you for the work you do preserving these records.

Sincerely,
