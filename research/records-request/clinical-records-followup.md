## Why this matters more than it used to

Larry cannot give the date he reached Camp Zama, and that date is now the single most valuable
missing fact in the KIA-misreport search (ledger #1, issue #9). Pacific Stars and Stripes
identified casualties **three to five days behind the event**, so a firm admission date, worked
back through the medevac chain he described on 2026-09-02, collapses the search window from a
fortnight of issues to two or three. **The clinical record answers it exactly** — an inpatient
record carries an admission and a discharge date.

The OMPF gives a weaker version of the same thing: **DA Form 20** (Enlisted Qualification
Record), item 33, is a chronological assignment record and should show his transfer to the unit
printed on the DD-214 — *"MHD USA MED COMD APO SF 96343 USARJ"*, the Medical Holding Detachment.
That is an **assignment** date, which need not equal the date he was admitted as a patient.

## If the clinical records genuinely do not survive

Fall back, in order:

1. **DA Form 20, item 33**, from the OMPF — the assignment date to the Medical Holding
   Detachment. Nearest available proxy.
2. **Morning reports** for that Medical Holding Detachment, USARJ, autumn 1965 — daily gains and
   losses, which would show the exact date he joined. NPRC holds Army morning reports; this is
   the same avenue as issue #27 and can be requested by unit and month.
3. **VA claim file** — the 2015 Agent Orange rating decision is already in the private archive
   and may cite the 1965 hospitalisation with dates, since service medical records are pulled
   into VA adjudication. **This one costs nothing and has not been checked** — worth reading the
   existing VA paperwork for a Camp Zama admission date before sending anything at all.
