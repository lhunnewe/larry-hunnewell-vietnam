## Why the C-file should contain it

For the 2015 ischemic-heart-disease claim, VA had to establish Vietnam service and review his
medical history. To adjudicate that, VA obtains the veteran's **service treatment records
(STRs)** and often the personnel file, and files them in the claims folder. **STRs are where an
inpatient episode at an Army hospital would appear** — and the 1965 hospitalisation for hepatitis
and malaria is exactly the sort of thing an adjudicator would have had in front of them.

It is not guaranteed. STRs are sometimes obtained only in part, and clinical (inpatient) records
are filed separately from outpatient STRs at NPRC and may never have reached VA. But the C-file
is free, it is the veteran's own record, and nothing else in the queue costs nothing.

## What to file

**VA Form 20-10206**, "Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) or Privacy Act (PA) Request."

> **Verify before sending** — form numbers and submission routes change, and this was drafted
> against knowledge that may be out of date. Check the current form and address at
> <https://www.va.gov/find-forms/> and the VA FOIA/Privacy pages, or call 1-800-827-1000.

**Submission options, easiest first:**

1. **Online via AccessVA / QuickSubmit** — usually the fastest, and returns an electronic C-file.
2. **VA.gov** — a signed-in veteran can request records directly.
3. **Mail** — to the VA Regional Office handling his claims, or the Evidence Intake Center.
   Historically the VA Records Management Center in St. Louis held paper C-files; most are now
   electronic in VBMS, so an electronic request is preferable.

**Larry must sign it**, as the veteran, or sign an authorisation naming his son. He does not use
a computer, so his son will do the filing; the signature still has to be his.

## What to ask for — wording

Do **not** ask only for "my C-file". Ask for the file **and name the specific documents**, because
a general request can come back as a partial release:

> I request a complete copy of my VA claims file (C-file) under the Privacy Act, including all
> documents in my electronic claims folder.
>
> I am specifically requesting the following, which I believe are contained in the file from the
> adjudication of my 2015 claim:
>
> 1. **All service treatment records (STRs)** obtained by VA, for the period **1963–1965**.
> 2. **Any record of inpatient hospitalisation in 1965**, in particular treatment at the
>    **U.S. Army Hospital, Camp Zama** (later the U.S. Army Medical Center, Japan), Sagami-Ōno,
>    Japan, for **hepatitis (jaundice) and malaria**, following medical evacuation from Vietnam.
> 3. **Any record of my assignment to the Medical Holding Detachment**, U.S. Army Medical
>    Command, APO SF 96343, U.S. Army Japan, shown as my last duty assignment on my DD-214.
> 4. **My separation physical examination**, conducted on or about 30 November 1965 at the
>    U.S. Army Personnel Center, Oakland, California.
> 5. **Any DA Form 20** (Enlisted Qualification Record) or other personnel record in the file.
>
> **The specific facts I am seeking are the dates of admission to and discharge from hospital in
> 1965.**
>
> I request the records in electronic form if available.

That last line matters — an electronic release is faster and free, where paper copies can be
charged for and take months.

## Which file number to use

**Use the number printed on the 2015 rating decision letter**, not the one Larry recalls. The two
differ by one digit; both are recorded in `private/notes/`, with the letter's number identified
as the one to put on forms. Getting this wrong is the most likely cause of a slow or misrouted
request.
