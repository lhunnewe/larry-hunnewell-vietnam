`research/site-audit-2026-09-14.md` rewrote the rendered fields (`name`, `aliases`/`alternateNames`,
`role`, `summary`) and the `researchNotes` of these records, so that the parked person pages can
come back safely. Reasons, in short: unit designations and other answers to open cold questions
(rule 6) out of every record field; machine output no longer counted as corroboration (rule 4);
paraphrase no longer quoted as Larry's words; stale next steps replaced with dated results;
private details out.

Below is each changed field **as it stood at commit `aeaf73d`**, verbatim, so no analysis is lost
and every change can be checked. It is the old text, not current findings: where it disagrees
with the record, the record is the later reading. Git history holds the same text.

## `data/people/gagne.json`

**role** (before):

**summary** (before):

Describing an attack on the Special Forces camp at Đồng Xoài, he said the man on the other radio was "dying from shot in the stomach," and then: "Gagne was with me and died, he was a french foreign Legionair." The same day he placed Gagne concretely: he ran the Special Forces armory, and it was Gagne who signed out the Thompson submachine gun that stands against Larry's bunk in VN-0082 and handed it to him. His words are held in data/recollections/, where the fidelity travels with them. On 2026-09-05 Larry added a rank and a distinction: "Sargent Gagne made Field Promotion to Lieutenant and that don't really happen. He was that good." And he places Gagne at his own first arrival on the mountain — "When I first got there (Nui Ba Den for the first time) with Gagne" — when a Cambodian chief wearing a necklace of thirty human ears came into the building where they were.

**researchNotes** (before):

> The name and its spelling are Larry's own, given on the telephone and checked with him more than once; on 2026-09-02 his son also recorded the pronunciation as "Gone-yay". THE FATE IS CONTRADICTED BY LARRY HIMSELF AND IS NOT RECONCILED HERE. On 2026-09-01 he said Gagne died; on 2026-09-02, asked directly, he said Gagne did not die and was alive when Larry was medevaced out (data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18259589.json). Nothing is corrected in either record. The practical consequence is large: the earlier account made this a casualty search, and the later one makes casualty files the wrong universe of records entirely — a man who survived Larry's tour would not appear in DCAS or on the Virtual Wall at all, and searching there would return a meaningless zero. Nationality moved too, but only partly: asked whether Gagne was French himself he said "Yes, pretty sure," then immediately widened it — "He could have been born in Germany or Belgium, I don't know." That is consistent with the Legion, which recruits foreigners by design and has historically enlisted men under assumed identities, and it means a French national record set is a reasonable first place to look but not a safe assumption. Also recorded, and unresolved as written: "He did seem to care much for Lowry. Lowry was an alcoholic and Gagne didn't seem to drink much that I noticed." The first sentence reads as though a negation dropped out in typing, which would reverse its sense; it is quoted as given and not repaired, and the relationship between these two men matters because Larry believes Lowry is the likely source of the erroneous KIA report (see data/people/sgt-lowry.json). 2026-09-05 (data/recollections/giscus-stories-c18308877.json, giscus-vn-0041-c18308745.json): a battlefield commission from sergeant to lieutenant is exactly the kind of thing that leaves paper — direct appointments were published in orders and are in the OMPF — so 'Gagne' as a sergeant AND as a lieutenant in the same detachment doubles the ways a roster can find him, and a commissioning in 1965 in a Special Forces detachment in III Corps is rare enough to be searchable on its own. Larry's 'that don't really happen' is his own assessment; he is right that it was uncommon. Note also that a French Foreign Legionnaire, as Larry describes him, could not have held a U.S. commission; either the Legion is his past, or one of the two descriptions is not literally so. Recorded, not reconciled. 2026-09-12 (giscus-vn-0013-c18415696), at VN-0013, the photograph of Larry with his Thompson beside his hooch: "It was mine and I got it from Gagne. He was in charge of all the weapons at Ton Son Nhut." The weapon and the man are the same as on 2026-09-02, but the place is not: that account had Gagne running the Special Forces armory, and the rest of Gagne's record puts him on the mountain. Recorded as given, not reconciled.
