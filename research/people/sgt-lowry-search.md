## Finding 1 — he is not in the casualty record, and that is the point

Every surname variant across the complete NARA DCAS national roll (the corpus built for ledger
#1 this morning, 54 jurisdictions):

**LOWERY** ×15, **LOWRY** ×5, **LOWREY** ×1 — twenty-one men in all.

**Not one died in 1964 or 1965 in a way that could be this man.** The earliest is Dalton Lowery
(USMC, 20 Dec 1966). The only 1965 death is **Capt Tyrrell Lowry, USAF, 18 Jun 1965, Portland
OR** — Air Force aircrew, remains not recovered, one of the 18 June 1965 losses; not a Special
Forces sergeant on a mountain. No Army Special Forces Lowry or Lowery died during or near
Larry's tour.

**This is consistent with Larry's account, which never says the man died** — and it carries a
methodological consequence worth stating plainly:

> **The casualty databases are the wrong tool for this man.** Every other person-search in this
> archive has run through memorial and casualty sources because those are the searchable dead.
> Lowry is presumptively **alive**, or died outside the war. He will be found, if at all, in
> **rosters, unit associations, and veteran registries** — not in a memorial.

## Finding 2 — the searchable handle is the detachment, not the name

Every previous attempt has searched a phonetic surname with two spellings. The camp now has a
designation, and it is a far better key.

From `data/places/nui-ba-den.json` (archive session 2026-09-01) and confirmed independently
today: the Special Forces camp on the mountain was **Detachment A-324**, opened **July 1964**,
grid **XT281582**, map sheet 6231-4.

And newly, from the 9th Infantry Regiment ("Manchus") site:

> "The United States Army Special Forces was headquartered out of **Ben Soi, A-321** but had unit
> **A-324** on the mountain comprising of about 30 men."

> A-324's responsibilities included **providing continuous radio relay for Special Forces and
> Vietnam Special Forces across III Corps**, advising on camp defence, and conducting security
> patrols. "In November 1967, the last two responsibilities were discontinued because the CIDG
> was pulled off the mountain and replaced by a company size security force from the 25th
> Infantry Division."

**Two things follow.**

1. **A-324's documented mission was continuous radio relay for the whole III Corps area.** That
   is independent corroboration of the thing Larry's account rests on — that the mountain was a
   radio site with Special Forces running it — and it matters for **#62** as much as for this
   question. The summit was a relay long before Granite Romeo Tango; it was simply an SF relay,
   not a signal-battalion one.
2. **A named archival target.** The page cites *"After Action Reports from both 125th Signal
   Battalion and Special Forces A-324."* **A-324 After Action Reports** is a request that can be
   made by name — add it to the NARA RG 472 effort (#27, #66) rather than filing separately.

**A caution, because the strength figures disagree.** "About 30 men" sits alongside Kelly's
official history, already cited in the place record, giving the October 1964 camp **1 officer,
5 enlisted and ~175 CIDG strikers**. The Manchus page is describing the **later** period — its
surrounding text is about 1967–68 — so the two are probably both right about different years.
**Do not quote "30 men" for 1964–65.** Larry's own "There was no one there from Special Forces
that day except Lowry" fits the half-team, not the thirty.

## Finding 3 — the rosters to search are probably the wrong group

This redirects the search materially. Per ARSOF history (*A Team Effort: Special Forces in
Vietnam, June–December 1964*):

- A-teams carried three-digit designations tied to their parent group.
- In 1964 the teams in Vietnam were rotating **TDY** detachments from the **1st Special Forces
  Group (Okinawa)** and the **7th Special Forces Group (Fort Bragg)**.
- **The 5th SFG deployed to Vietnam only in late 1964**, "to assume command and control of
  Special Forces throughout the country."

So a sergeant on that mountain in 1964–early 1965 is quite likely a **1st or 7th SFG man on a
six-month rotation**, not a 5th SFG man. Searching 5th SFG rosters and the 5th SFG veteran
community — the obvious move, and the one this archive's notes have implied — may be aimed at
the wrong organisation for the relevant window.

*(Recorded with its limit: ARSOF names A-321 as a 7th SFG team at **Buon Brieng**, a different
camp. That does not establish that the A-321 at Ben Soi was 7th SFG, and it is not claimed here.
The general rotation point stands on its own.)*
