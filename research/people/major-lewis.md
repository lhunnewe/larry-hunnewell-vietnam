# Major Lewis — identity notes

**Status:** unresolved. Larry's identification (VN-0016 Memories comment, 2026-08-15):
"This is major Lewis. We are flying in Aircraft Rosethorn 007."

## The helmet-lettering tension — DO NOT raise with Larry

The 2026-08-09 vision survey read the lettering on the pilot's helmet visor cover as
approximately **"ESTES"** (uncertain machine reading, flagged for magnification). That does
not match "Lewis." **Magnification pass 2026-08-15**
(research/magnification/vn-0016-helmet-lettering.png): the lettering is partially occluded
by the visor lift knob — "ES…"-like strokes left of the knob and a clear final "S"; the
reading stays **consistent with ESTES but not conclusive**. Possible reconciliations, none
established:

1. The machine reading is wrong — five low-contrast letters on a curved visor cover; a
   magnified pass on the original scan should come first.
2. Borrowed gear — flight helmets were pooled or swapped often enough that the name on the
   visor cover need not be the wearer's.
3. Larry's name is a memory slip and the pilot is an Estes; or "Lewis" is right and Estes
   was a different crew member whose helmet this was.

Per project rule, this hypothesis stays in `research/` and out of rendered fields.
Interview approach: ask **open** questions only — "Do you remember other pilots or crew on
Rosethorn 007 besides Major Lewis and Captain Guegler?" If a name like Estes surfaces
unprompted, that is strong signal.

- Cross-reference with Captain Guegler (research: `data/people/captain-guegler.json`) —

## 2026-09-12 — the helmet was read, and Lewis was withdrawn

**The probe did not fire in the way it was designed to, and that is the result to record.**
The plan above was to ask open questions and wait for "Estes" to surface unprompted. What
happened instead: at VN-0016 his son said it looked like there was a name on the helmet, and
Larry read it (`data/recollections/giscus-vn-0016-c18415765.json`, transcribed):

> oh his last name is Estes, I was thinking his name was Lewis. I think he was National Guard
> and probably got called up. Lewis must be somebody else. Geutler was the guy flying possibly
> to the left of Estes. Usually the guy looking back is not flying the plane and he was talking
> to me.

What that establishes:

- **The lettering reads ESTES.** Two independent readings now agree: the machine survey and
  magnification, and Larry reading the photograph. That settles reconciliation (1) above:
  the machine reading was not wrong.
- **It does not establish the wearer.** Reconciliation (2), borrowed gear, is untouched by a
  second reading of the same visor. It is weakened only by what Larry added from memory: a
  name he connects to a person (National Guard, called up, talking to him over his shoulder).
  That association arrived *after* he read the name, so it may be memory prompted by the
  name, or memory of the man.
- **Lewis is withdrawn from this frame by Larry himself** ("Lewis must be somebody else").
  That is his inference from the reading, not a memory of a second man. `major-lewis.json`
  is kept; the photograph link is removed (VN-0016 now names `estes`), as with Stanton on
  VN-0026.
- **The pilot at the controls** is, per Larry, not the man looking back, and "possibly"
  Geutler. Not linked.

Consequences: the Rosethorn 007 crew question in the guide is spent as worded (it named
Lewis). The new open question is **Estes**, ledger #37, issue #155; #63 (Lewis) loses its
photograph. The 1LT Lewis collision noted in ledger #28 still stands, now against a name
with no face.

**National Guard, not checked:** no Army National Guard units were mobilized for Vietnam
before 1968, so the testable readings are an individual Guardsman on active duty or a man
with prior Guard service who was in the active Army by 1964–65.
