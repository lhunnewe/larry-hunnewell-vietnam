# Hunnewell family in the newspapers — sweeps of 2026-08-16/17

Run on the family's newspapers.com trial after the family supplied the 1962–65 home
address (8815 12th Avenue, Inglewood, Calif.) and the parents' names: **Lester Arthur
Hunnewell** and **Geraldine Ruth Hunnewell, née Grosvenor**. Newspapers.com image IDs
cited so items can be re-opened directly. Living-relative details kept minimal.

  **Mrs. Lester Hunnewell, secretary**; and Miss Rita E. Ackerman, treasurer... the

- Context: Morningside is the Inglewood neighborhood; the Wave was the LA
  southside/Inglewood community weekly. So while Larry was in high school and then in
  Vietnam, his mother was a club officer whose meetings made the society page.

  1974–1983: zero.
- The extracted **birth-records index** for the Breeze read end-to-end: Dec 1974 – Feb

  scanned): no Hunnewell, and no "Mr. and Mrs. Larry" candidate. Keyword "Leroy" against

- Reading of the columns: the Breeze printed per-hospital lists (Torrance Memorial,
  South Bay Hospital, Little Company of Mary, Memorial Hospital of Hawthorne) with a
  2–6-week lag. Conclusion: **the family likely didn't submit these two births, or the

  Dec 1974 is unknown). The negative is as solid as the digitized record allows.

  Der Wyk, David Zimbardo, Gary Prarat, Steve Canfield, Fred Doerges, **Larry

  the same Manchester-Avenue neighborhood where his mother's Toastmistress club met a
  decade later.
- *South Gate Press*, **Sep 1, 1957**, p. 1 (image 744618031, **clip 204023232**), now
  read in full — "**San Vincente Accident**": "...**Larry Hunnewell of Inglewood** was
  shaken up shortly after 4:15 p.m. when his **motorcycle collided with a car** driven
  by Utah Sutterfield, 48, of 9401 San Miguel avenue. Hunnewell was traveling east on
  **Firestone** and the Sutterfield car was heading south on **San Vincente**.
  **Hunnewell refused medical treatment at the scene.**" — Larry at ~16, front page.
- *The Daily Breeze* (Torrance), **Jul 10, 1959**, p. 3 (image 1094385223, **clip
  204023462**), full text: "An **Inglewood motorcyclist** is being treated today in
  Daniel Freeman Hospital for a concussion and face and head cuts, following a traffic
  accident yesterday in **Hawthorne**. **Thrown Out** — **Larry B. Hunnewell, 18**, was
  thrown to the pavement at **Crenshaw Blvd. and 120th St.** after his motorcycle
  collided with a vehicle driven by William T. Payne, 52, of 3802 W. 119th St.,
  Hawthorne. **Hunnewell was transported to Hawthorne Community Hospital for initial
  treatment and x-rays.** It first was feared the victim suffered a skull fracture and
  fractures of the right elbow, right leg, left collarbone and spine. Daniel Freeman
  officials said Hunnewell's condition today is 'good.'" *Caution: middle initial
  printed "B." (Larry's is D — Dennis); everything else fits — Inglewood, motorcycle, and
  the age: 18 in July 1959 matches Larry's documented 1940 birth exactly. Treat as
  strongly-supported, confirm at breakfast — "tell me about your motorcycle days in
  Inglewood" is now a Tier 3 interview question.*

## Montana Dart Co. — Larry and Betty's 1980s business (found 2026-08-17)

The 1986 "Betty Hunnewell" hits turn out to document a family manufacturing business:

- *The Billings Gazette*, **Mon Jan 20, 1986**, p. 5-A (image 413464416, **clip
  204022765**) — AP wire feature, "**Dart firm finds big demand**": "BIGFORK (AP) — The
  upstart **Montana Dart Manufacturing Co.** here is in the enviable position of having
  too many customers. 'I didn't have any confidence in these things when we first
  started,' says **Larry Hunnewell, who started the small metalworking firm east of
  Bigfork**. 'But now they are just going like crazy.' Each week the company produces
  about 1,000 'barrels' — the thick midsection of a barroom dart... 'I just talked to
  one of our salesmen over in Seattle... He thinks we can sell 30,000 of these a month
  if we can make them that fast.' An apparent nationwide dart craze and difficulty
  getting high-quality dart components from traditional sources in England has forced
  several U.S. dart distributors to seek a new source of projectiles... Montana Dart's
  barrels are made of brass, nickel, nickel plate or silver, and are produced in nine
  different styles **on old Swiss-made lathes**. Hunnewell says he's counting on getting
  a new **$50,000 computerized lathe from Japan**... 'Our productivity will go up 200
  percent right off the bat'... A set of three darts with their barrels costs between
  $18 and $42 retail. The firm's most popular model is the '**Montana missile**,' with a
  nickel-plated barrel... Oddly enough, Montana Dart's products are not yet available in
  Montana. 'We just haven't had time to sell them locally,' says **Betty Hunnewell**."
  The same wire story ran in the Missoulian (Jan 19, p. 39) and Montana Standard (Jan
  20, p. 5).
- *The Daily Inter Lake*, **Sun Aug 24, 1986**, p. 37 — legal notice: "Application for
  a Conditional Use Permit by **Betty Hunnewell** for property described as **202½
  Meridian Road, Lot 1, Block 2, Meridian Addition of Kalispell**... would allow a Home
  Occupation Use for an **Electronic Parts**..." (hearing Sep 8, 1986, City Hall).
- *The Daily Inter Lake*, **Tue Sep 9, 1986**, p. 4 (image 1076922889, **clip
  204022375**) — "**Home-business question sparks debate**" by Roger Hopkins: neighbors
  of the Meridian Addition urged denial; "Real estate agent Larry Lee said the
  prospective tenants want to use the cement block building on the property for their
  business, the Montana Dart Co. which they want to relocate from Bigfork. He said in
  addition to manufacturing game darts for out-of-state sales... **Larry and Betty
  Hunnewell contract with local manufacturing firms as JBM Inc. and Semitool Inc.,
  tooling small parts for use in their operations.**" (Semitool was Kalispell's big
  semiconductor-equipment maker.) Opponent Jeff Houston: "Even though it's called a home
  occupation, it's still an industrial use of the land," noting the **metal lathes**;
  Lee: machine noise is "at the level of an air conditioner."
- *The Daily Inter Lake*, **Thu Oct 16, 1986**, p. 4 (image 1076947447, **clip
  204022238**) — "**Home-business permit OK'd**": "On a 4-1 vote, the board approved the
  permit for **Larry and Betty Hunnewell** to operate a small manufacturing operation.
  The Hunnewells own **Montana Dart Co. in Bigfork** and plan to move their operation to
  the new site... at the southwest corner of Meridian and Second Street West," with city
  attorney Glen Neier finding the use would have "no adverse effect."
- *Ask the family*: memories of the dart factory, the "Montana missile," the Semitool
  contract work, and what became of the business before the family's late-1980s years.
