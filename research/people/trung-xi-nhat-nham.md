## The reading: this is probably a rank, not a full name

The rendering is his son's transcription of a sound, not a spelling Larry gave letter by
letter. Read as Vietnamese, it separates:

- **Trung sĩ** (transcribed "Trung Xi") — *sergeant*.
- **Trung sĩ nhất** (transcribed "Trung Xi Nhat") — *sergeant first class / master sergeant*,
  the senior NCO grade in the ARVN and in the CIDG structures Special Forces worked through.
- **Nham** — what is left over, and the only part that can be a personal name.

So the phrase Larry remembers as a name most likely means **"Master Sergeant Nham"**, in the
same way "Sarge" attached to the monkey. If that is right, the archive is holding a rank badge
and one syllable of a name, and every search built on the whole string as a surname will
return nothing forever.

**This is a hypothesis from the shape of the words, not a finding.** Nothing has been searched
yet, and Larry has not been asked.

## Why it matters more than most name questions

Larry has named many men in passing. This is the first he describes as **assigned to protect
him**, and the description is unusually specific in three ways that could each be checked
independently:

1. **Nine languages.** Interpreters of that calibre at a Special Forces site were few.
2. **A religious vocation** — "a warrior monk." Núi Bà Đen was a pilgrimage mountain with an
   active temple complex, which makes the description internally coherent rather than exotic;
   Larry independently mentions the temple on the trail around the east side in VN-0057.
3. **Placed at a specific documented event** — the wreck in VN-0148, which Larry says killed
   seven men and burned for three hours.

## How to ask, when the moment comes

Open, and nothing supplied:

> *"The man standing by the burnt helicopter — the interpreter. What did you all call him?
> Was that his name, or his rank? Where was he from?"*

If he separates the rank himself, that is independent confirmation of the reading above. If we
give him "Master Sergeant Nham" first, his agreement proves nothing.
