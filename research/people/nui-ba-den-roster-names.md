# The names on the rocks — two of them are now documented men

**Source:** Stephen Sherman, *U.S. Army Special Forces Order of Battle, Vietnam Theater
(1957–1975)*, RADIX Press 1995, Part 2 of 3 (VNCA item `4030205002`, 134 pp,
copyright-restricted, read through the Reading Room portal 2026-09-01).
`data/sources/radix-sf-order-of-battle`.

**Bearing on:** VN-0035 and VN-0133, the roster rocks Larry photographed at Núi Bà Đen;
ledger #4 and issue #12 (Sgt. Lowry); the interview probe about the painted names.

**Handling:** the names were read off Larry's own photographs, so they are not secret from
him. What must not reach him before he is asked is **who these men were** — being told
"Garvey commanded the detachment" would reshape whatever he remembers. The identifications
below stay in `research/`. The interview question stays as it is: does he remember the names.

---

## The camp itself, in print

The order of battle's entry is the most specific description of the site this project has
found:

> **Nui Ba Den — 324 — Tay Ninh Province / Phú Khương District — XT281582 —
> MISSION: RADIO RELAY**

Not CIDG. Not a strike camp. **The camp's stated mission was the radio relay** — which is
the job Larry describes doing there. A note under the roster records its origin:

> "MAJ Lamar (S-4, B-220) was given mission to **set up AN-19 radio relay site**"

("AN-19" is almost certainly a garbled AN/GRC-19, the period HF radio set.) The site was
established July 1964, two months before Larry reached Vietnam.

## Garvey and Rewerts

Larry photographed rocks painted with **Rewerts, Garvey, Kirby, Captain Lynch, Dean,
Willcut**. Two of those are now documented Special Forces officers serving in Tây Ninh
province in 1964:

- **GARVEY, JAMES E, 1LT — commanded the Núi Bà Đen detachment, July to September 1964**,
  TDY from A5/122B. A man who commanded the relay site itself is exactly whose name would be
  painted on a rock there. **Confidence: strongly-supported** as the Garvey of the rock,
  short of a photograph of the painted name matched to a service record.
- **REWERTS, DAVID G, CPT** — appears twice: at a camp on the river that served as an FOB
  for Trảng Sụp, June–July 1964 (A5/122 TDY), and commanding at **Trảng Sụp**, Tây Ninh —
  B-32's own base — 25 June to November 1964. **Confidence: plausible-to-strongly-supported.**

A detail that ties them together and raises both: **Garvey and Rewerts were both TDY from
A5/122** — the same parent team, in the same province, in the same months. The rocks
plausibly carry the names of one TDY rotation.

### The weaker three

- **"Captain Lynch."** A **LYNCH, MICHAEL J** appears as 1LT at **Bu Dop** (Phước Long,
  not Tây Ninh) May–November 1964, and later as a captain at Kannack in 1966. Right rank
  eventually, wrong province in the right year. **Plausible at best; do not assert.**
- **Willcut.** The only Willcut in the work is **CPT Roger E. Willcut, 5th Special Forces
  Group Signal Officer**. A group signal officer's name on the rocks of a radio relay site
  is an attractive fit, but the dates shown for that appointment run 1966 and later.
  **Recorded as a candidate with a date problem.**
- **Kirby** and **Dean**: **no match** anywhere in either part of the order of battle.

### And a negative worth having

**No Lowry and no Lowery appear anywhere in Part 1 or Part 2** — not as a detachment
commander, not as a team sergeant, not in the group staff lists. Scope, stated so this is
not over-read: this work rosters **officers and senior NCOs**, not every enlisted man on a
team. Larry describes Sgt. Lowry as the Special Forces man at the camp, not necessarily its
team sergeant. So this is a real but **narrow** negative for issue #12 — it rules the name
out of the leadership rosters, not out of the detachment.

## The Bu Dop chain closes

Independently of the rocks, this document confirms the story behind the July 1965 probe.
The Bu Dop entry (Phước Long Province / Bu Đức District, YU97452909) lists:

> **NUGENT, RICHARD O, CPT — 03/??/65 – 07/20/65 — A5/341A (PCS) — "Camp overrun."**
> OLIVAZ, JACINTO E, 1LT — 07/22/65 — fm XO/CO

The MACV *Observer* of 24 July 1965 (`data/sources/macv-observer-1965-07-24`) reported the
attack on Bu Dop **shortly after 1 a.m. on 20 July 1965**, named **Capt. Richard O. Nugent**
among those carried to safety, and quoted the camp's communications sergeant saying he
"contacted the Black Virgin Mountain radio station."

Two independent sources — a compiled order of battle and MACV's own newspaper — now agree on
the date, the camp, and its commander, and one of them says that camp called the mountain.
**One discrepancy kept:** the order of battle says "camp overrun"; the *Observer* says the
attempt to overrun it failed and the defenders held. The newspaper is contemporary and
specific; the order of battle is a compiler's shorthand written decades later. Neither is
adjusted to fit the other.

None of this establishes that Larry took the call. The probe in the interview guide stands
exactly as written, and none of these details may be supplied to him first.

## Two more names in Larry's window

- **LEWIS, 1LT — commanding at Núi Bà Đen from 20 April 1965**, PCS from A-325A. This is the
  Special Forces officer commanding the camp during the months Larry was detached there.
  **He is not Larry's "Major Lewis," the pilot of Rosethorn 007** (issue #63, VN-0016):
  different rank, different job, an infantry detachment commander rather than an aviator.
  The coincidence of surname is recorded so that a future session neither merges them nor
  re-discovers the collision as if it were new.
- **CPT Peter W. Van Dine, A-311** — listed among TDY increments at "Don Phuoc / Trang Sup /
  **Nui Ba Den**," **April to October 1965**, which spans Larry's whole time at the mountain.

## Also settled in passing

**MAJ Galen "Mike" Radke** is listed taking **B-32 Tây Ninh in January 1965**. That matters
because two camp lists read earlier the same day gave B-32's start as March 1965, against
Kelly's official history saying January. The commander roster now supports **January 1965**,
and the 1967 5th SFG report's "MAJ BADKE, TAY NINH" is the same name mis-OCR'd. The
discrepancy is not closed, but the weight has moved back to Kelly.
