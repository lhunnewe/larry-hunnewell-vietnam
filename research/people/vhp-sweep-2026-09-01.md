# Veterans History Project — first sweep, 2026-09-01

Opened by the accident recorded in `../places/vhp-chivers-a324.md`. Ledger #20, issue #70.

**Everything below is published archival description from the Library of Congress.** Names
and collection numbers are theirs, public, and citable. **No contact details are recorded
here and none should be**, per this archive's privacy rule on living people.

## Method, so this is repeatable

The LOC website rate-limits browsing and served 503s within a few page loads. **The JSON API
does not.** Every search below was run as:

```
https://www.loc.gov/collections/veterans-history-project-collection/?q=<term>&fo=json&c=100
```

with a browser User-Agent, then filtered locally. Each result carries a full
`item.service_history` block — dates of service, branch, rank, unit, location — so **service
dates can be filtered client-side without opening a single item page.** That is the trick
that made this sweep possible in one pass.

## The mountain, complete: only one man overlaps Larry's year

Of the **12 collections** that mention Núi Bà Đen or Black Virgin Mountain, service dates
break down as:

- **1962–69 — Terona Chivers**, Sergeant, **5th Special Forces Group** — the A-324
  photograph. The only Special Forces veteran of the mountain in the VHP whose service spans
  Larry's year.
- **1964–69 — Gary Stanford Hauge**, CW3, 74th Aviation Company; 205th Assault Support
  Helicopter Company. Dates overlap; the Vietnam portion is unspecified.
- **All ten others begin in 1966 or later** — 25th Infantry Division, 196th Light Infantry
  Brigade, 199th LIB, 1st Cavalry, Air Force reconnaissance.

That distribution is itself a finding, and it matches everything else learned today: **the
mountain only became a crowded American place after Larry left.** In 1964–65 it was a half
A-team and a radio relay; the veterans who wrote it into their oral histories were mostly
there for the war it became.

**A structural point that matters more than any of these searches.** The Veterans History
Project is built from **veterans who lived to record an interview**, or whose families later
deposited their materials. **A man who died in 1965 is very unlikely to be in it.** So for
**Gagne** specifically this was always a low-probability search, and its failure says almost
nothing. The casualty databases and the 5th SFG records remain the right places, once we
know from Larry whether Gagne was American — see issue #68.

The zeros for Guegler, Schwartzkoff and Totosz are worth having but are also narrow: all
three names reach us only as Larry's phonetic renderings, and the VHP indexes the spelling
its donors gave. A miss here is a miss on *that spelling*, not on the man.

# Fifth pass, 2026-09-02 — the camps, the B-detachment, the job, and the names

Everything the fourth pass left unqueried is now queried. **Two new men worth reading, one
detachment-level surprise, and a great many honest zeros.**

## Three method faults found and fixed first

The earlier passes were sound but the script had three defects, all of which suppressed
results rather than inventing them. They are recorded because a later session will otherwise
re-inherit them.

1. **Unquoted multi-word terms token-match.** `Loc Ninh` returned 183 hits, most of them
   *Tay Ninh*. Quoted, `"Loc Ninh"` returns **35**. Every phrase search must be quoted.
2. **Years were being scraped from the whole `service_history` block**, which contains
   `"war_or_conflict": "Vietnam War, 1961-1975"`. That string gave every Vietnam veteran a
   1961–1975 span and pushed real matches into the discard pile. Years must come only from
   `dates_of_service`.
3. **Records with no `dates_of_service` were silently dropped.** VHP dates are donor-supplied
   and often absent; the very first camp query returned such a record. Undated records are now
   kept in their own bucket and judged by hand.

Fault 2 in particular means **the third and fourth passes' "long career" exclusions should be
treated as soft**, not as clean eliminations.

### The one that matters — Harvey E. Stewart

**[98375](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.98375/)** · Army, **Colonel** · online **video**

His Vietnam service block reads:

> **`"battles_campaigns": "Dong Xoai"`** · **`"unit_of_service": "Military Assistance Command
> Vietnam (MACV); 118th Assault Helicopter Company"`**

**This is the only record found anywhere that holds both halves of the open unit question at
once** — the **118th** Larry calls his outfit, and **Đồng Xoài**, the battle he says he
listened to on the radio. The 118th Assault Helicopter Company did fly in support of Đồng
Xoài in June 1965, so the combination is historically coherent rather than coincidental.

**What is not established.** His Vietnam dates are **blank**; only the Korea block is dated,
and 1951–1975 covers a whole career. `battles_campaigns` is donor-supplied free text, not a
records citation. So this places a man in the right unit at the right battle **by his own
account**, and nothing more, until someone watches the interview.

### Jesse James Hodges — eliminated, cleanly

**[64183](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.64183/)** · 5th SFG, Đồng Xoài. His own service

### Florencio Berumen — the sister camp's commander

**[43703](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.43703/)** · Army, 1957–1979 · online **audio** ·
**Detachment A-331, 5th Special Forces**, Lộc Ninh. His note: *"served as a commander of a
Special Forces unit located at Loc Ninh, near the Cambodian border."* **A-331 is a sister
A-camp under the same C-3 that ran Núi Bà Đen.** His Vietnam years are undated inside a
22-year career, so he may be nowhere near 1965 — but a commander describing how a III Corps
A-camp was run, supplied and communicated with is worth an hour whatever the year.

## Detachment B-32 — two men, and both are three years late

- **Harold Raymond Winton** — [116237](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.116237/) (video,

**Both are too late, and the campaign lists say so.** Winton's are Counteroffensive Phase III,

**So the finding is a negative, and it is worth stating plainly: the Veterans History Project
holds no B-32 veteran from Larry's year.** Nobody in ~99,000 collections was at his
headquarters detachment in 1964–65.

They remain worth reading at a lower tier, for the *shape* of the thing rather than the
period: how B-32 ran its A-camps out of Trảng Sụp, what it did about the mountain, and whether
either man mentions the relay site as an inherited fixture. Winton is a lieutenant colonel
interviewed by Joe Galloway — likely the most substantial interview surfaced by this sweep.

## The job — a real negative

- `"flight following"` — **0 hits.** The phrase Larry uses for his own work appears nowhere in
  the Veterans History Project.
- `"radio relay"` — **26 hits**, and **not one is III Corps in 1964–65.** They are Air Force

That is worth having. His trade, in his place, in his year, is not represented in the largest
oral-history collection in the United States. It is a small piece of evidence for something
this archive already suspected: **the mountain relay in 1964–65 was a handful of men, and
almost none of them recorded anything.**

**The Veterans History Project is, for practical purposes, exhausted for this archive.** The

in one record, and the strongest new lead of this pass — and **Florencio Berumen**, the A-331

**What is left, and it is thin:** watching the interviews. Every collection named in this file
and in #71 is **online and free** — Stewart, Winton, Mosier, Gudermuth, Massey, Chivers and
Fell as video; Berumen and one Winton record as audio. **No further searching is required to
begin reading.**
