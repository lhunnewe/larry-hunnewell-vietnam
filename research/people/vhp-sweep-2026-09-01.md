# Veterans History Project — first sweep, 2026-09-01

Opened by the accident recorded in `../places/vhp-chivers-a324.md`. Ledger #20, issue #70.

**Everything below is published archival description from the Library of Congress.** Names
and collection numbers are theirs, public, and citable. **No contact details are recorded
here and none should be**, per this archive's privacy rule on living people.

## Method, so this is repeatable

The LOC website rate-limits browsing and served 503s within a few page loads. **The JSON API
does not.** Every search below was run as:

```
https://www.loc.gov/collections/veterans-history-project-collection/?q=<term>&fo=json&c=100
```

with a browser User-Agent, then filtered locally. Each result carries a full
`item.service_history` block — dates of service, branch, rank, unit, location — so **service
dates can be filtered client-side without opening a single item page.** That is the trick
that made this sweep possible in one pass.

## The mountain, complete: only one man overlaps Larry's year

Of the **12 collections** that mention Núi Bà Đen or Black Virgin Mountain, service dates
break down as:

- **1962–69 — Terona Chivers**, Sergeant, **5th Special Forces Group** — the A-324
  photograph. The only Special Forces veteran of the mountain in the VHP whose service spans
  Larry's year.
- **1964–69 — Gary Stanford Hauge**, CW3, 74th Aviation Company; 205th Assault Support
  Helicopter Company. Dates overlap; the Vietnam portion is unspecified.
- **All ten others begin in 1966 or later** — 25th Infantry Division, 196th Light Infantry
  Brigade, 199th LIB, 1st Cavalry, Air Force reconnaissance.

That distribution is itself a finding, and it matches everything else learned today: **the
mountain only became a crowded American place after Larry left.** In 1964–65 it was a half
A-team and a radio relay; the veterans who wrote it into their oral histories were mostly
there for the war it became.

## Second pass, same day — the names Larry gives, and the unit candidates

### Searching by name: four clean zeros and one non-match

| Name searched | Hits in the whole VHP | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Guegler** | **0** | Nothing, anywhere, in ~99,000 collections |
| **Schwartzkoff** | **0** | Nothing under Larry's spelling |
| **Totosz** | **0** | Nothing |
| **Gagne** | 5 total, 1 Vietnam-era | **Not our man** — Ludger L. Gagne, 1959–79, 20th Engineer Brigade, Vietnam and Korea. Wrong kind of unit, wrong shape of service. |
| **Lowry** | 771 (word matches "Lowry Air Force Base" too) | No 1964–65 Vietnam Lowry in the first 100 |
| **Lowery** | 53 | No 1965 Special Forces Lowery |

**A structural point that matters more than any of these searches.** The Veterans History
Project is built from **veterans who lived to record an interview**, or whose families later
deposited their materials. **A man who died in 1965 is very unlikely to be in it.** So for
**Gagne** specifically this was always a low-probability search, and its failure says almost
nothing. The casualty databases and the 5th SFG records remain the right places, once we
know from Larry whether Gagne was American — see issue #68.

The zeros for Guegler, Schwartzkoff and Totosz are worth having but are also narrow: all
three names reach us only as Larry's phonetic renderings, and the VHP indexes the spelling
its donors gave. A miss here is a miss on *that spelling*, not on the man.

| **Steve Lee Etzel** | [124143](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.124143/) | 1964–1993 | Assault Helicopter Company, 145th Aviation Battalion |
| James Arthur Kilgore | [103087](https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.103087/) | 1949–1979 | 118th Aviation Company; Vietnam |

**Gudermuth is the one.** 1964–1967, the **118th**, under the **145th**, in Vietnam — the two
units this archive has argued about for a month, in one man's service record, across exactly
Larry's window. Whatever he recorded is the closest thing to a witness the unit question has
ever had.

**Fell matters for a different reason.** The **68th Assault Helicopter Company** is what the
UTT became. His service starts in 1965, so he arrives as Larry leaves, but he is inside the
other leading candidate.
