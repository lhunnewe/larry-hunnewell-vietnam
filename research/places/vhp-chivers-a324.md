# A stranger's photograph captioned "A-324" — the Veterans History Project, 2026-09-01

Found while checking whether the 1968 boulder film had escaped into commercial newsfilm
archives. It had not. This came up instead, and it is worth more.

## The find

**Terona Chivers Collection**, Veterans History Project, American Folklife Center, Library of
Congress — **AFC/2001/001/6049**, <https://www.loc.gov/item/afc2001001.06049/>

- **Sgt. Terona Chivers**, U.S. Army, **1962–1969**
- Service units recorded: 3rd Battalion, 506th Infantry Regiment, 101st Airborne Division;
  **5th Special Forces Group**
- Materials: **11 photographs** (collected 1962–69), one map, and a **three-part video oral
  history** recorded 11 December 2002 — the video and images are **online and free**

One of his photographs is catalogued with this caption:

> **"[Front]: Aerial view of Nui Ba Den (Black Lady) Mountain A-324 during air-drop.
> [December 25, 1965]"**

## Why this matters

**A veteran, unconnected to this project, captioned his own photograph of the mountain with
the detachment designation "A-324."**

Everything the archive knows about A-324 so far comes from compiled reference works — the
position index, two camp lists, and the RADIX order of battle
(`nui-ba-den-locations.md`, `../people/nui-ba-den-roster-names.md`). Those are good sources,
but they are all *compilations*, written decades later by researchers reading records. This
is different in kind: **a man who was there, writing the designation himself, as the name of
the place.** It is the first evidence that A-324 was not merely a filing convention but what
people on the ground called it.

And the date — **25 December 1965** — is three and a half months after Larry was medevaced.
Same detachment, same mountain, the same year. It is the closest photograph in time and place
to Larry's own that this project has found in anyone else's hands.

## What is not being claimed

- **He is not connected to Larry** on any evidence. Different units on paper, and nothing
  places them together.
- **His wound is not Larry's story.** The collection summary says Chivers was "shot in the
  stomach" and evacuated to Alaska; Larry, describing the Đồng Xoài attack, said the man on
  the other radio was "dying from shot in the stomach." **These are different men, different
  events, and Chivers survived.** Recorded here only so a later session does not collide them
  and think it has found something.
- Nothing here corroborates Larry's service. It corroborates the **designation** and shows
  the mountain in his year through someone else's camera.

## The bigger opening

The Veterans History Project holds roughly **99,000 collections**, is free, and is
name-, unit- and keyword-searchable. This archive has never searched it. A veteran who
served at Núi Bà Đen in 1964–65 — or in Detachment A-324, B-32, or the 5th SFG in Tây Ninh —
may have deposited photographs, letters or a recorded interview there, and any of them could
name the men Larry remembers.

That is a free research avenue as large as the Texas Tech one, opened by accident.
