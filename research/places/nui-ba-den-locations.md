# Núi Bà Đen — grid references, the detachment, and the French fort

**Source:** William Kindred's veteran-compiled index of fire support bases and landing zones,
VNCA item `25720204001`, 525 pp (`data/sources/kindred-fsb-lz-index`). Read 2026-09-01.
Entries are compiled from unit reports, each citing its source document — well-sourced
secondary data, not a primary record. Latitude/longitude and grids are as printed.

## The map sheet, which is the immediately actionable part

Every Núi Bà Đen entry sits on **1:50,000 map sheet 6231-4**. That is the number to send
VNCA to obtain the two **1:25,000 Pictomap L8020** halves, which are not online and are
emailed on request by sheet number plus N or S (see `../records-request/vnca/README.md`):

> **Request: Pictomap sheets 6231-4 N and 6231-4 S.**

A 1:25,000 pictorial sheet is the best available means of naming the settlements around the
mountain — including whatever village stood north of it in 1965.

## The Special Forces camp has a designation now

| Entry | Grid | Lat/long | Notes as printed |
|---|---|---|---|
| SF Nui Ba Den | XT281581 | — | "SF Det **A-325A, A-324**. Opened **Jul64**." |
| SF Nui Ba Den | XT281582 | 11.3824, 106.1702 | "aka Black Virgin Mountain, Hill 986. **SF Det A-324 Jul64–Oct70.** Signal facility overrun 13May68." |
| SF Hill 986 | XT281582 | 11.3822, 106.1700 | "**SF (and others) radio relay.** Attacked 13May68. US 19 KIA, facility destroyed." |
| Ps Upper Pagoda | XT285580 | 11.3806, 106.1739 | "Nui Ba Den mountain. **Jump-off point for SF patrols.**" |

Two things this archive did not have before:

1. **A detachment number.** Until now the camp was "a half A-team" from Kelly's official
   history. It is **Detachment A-324** (with A-325A also listed), opened **July 1964** —
   which is to say, it was two months old when Larry arrived in country and about a year old
   when he was detached to it. This converts every future records request from a description
   into a designation: ask NARA for **Det A-324, Company A (or the Tây Ninh B-32 area), 5th
   Special Forces Group, 1964–65**, not for "the CIDG camp at Núi Bà Đen."
2. **Independent confirmation that the summit was a radio relay in the SF period**, before
   the 121st Signal Battalion's "Granite Romeo Tango" arrived in February 1966. That is the
   window Larry describes, and it had been the thinnest part of the documentary record.

The **13 May 1968** attack that destroyed the signal facility (19 US KIA) is the event that
dominates every online search for the mountain. It is not Larry's period and not his story;
the caution already in `../events/450-civilians-nui-ba-den.md` stands.

## A French fort north of the mountain

Larry describes "the French fort on the back side of Nui Ba Den," where Sgt. Lowry was when
the accusation was made. The index carries one documented French fort in the area:

> **LZ Paul** — XT281685, 11.4756 / 106.1706, sheet 6231-4 — "2/12th Inf 12Apr67. Op
> Junction City. **Old French Fort north of Nui Ba Den.**"

**Correction and expansion, same day.** The archive's reference desk pointed at a newer
edition of this index than the one first read — **item 25720206001, September 2024, 553 pp**
(the January 2023 edition, 25720204001, was read first; its Núi Bà Đen entries are
identical). The newer edition shows that LZ Paul is not an isolated entry but one of a
**whole cluster of positions on sheet 6231-4 all called "French Fort,"** occupied by many
different units between 1966 and 1971:

| Name as used | Grid | Cited from |
|---|---|---|
| FB French Fort | XT274686 | 2/25th ID, 10 Nov 66, Op Attleboro |
| FB Saint Barbara — "aka **The French Fort**, FSB Bao Co" | XT275684 | 25th ID, 1st ACD, '68 |
| FB French Fort | XT277686 | 11th ACR HQ, 22 Feb 67, Op Junction City |
| **LZ Paul** — "**Old French Fort north of Nui Ba Den**" | XT281685 | 2/12th Inf, 12 Apr 67 |
| FB French Fort | XT281684 | B/2/35th Arty, 21 Mar 67 |
| Ps Plenitude — "Also **French Fort**, St Barbara" | XT289689 | 25th ID forward supply area |

So there is **one prominent, real, repeatedly-used French fort north of the mountain**,
around XT27–28/68–69, known to every American unit that passed through War Zone C. It is
the only such landmark on the sheet.

**Still a candidate, not an identification**, for two reasons that pull in opposite
directions:

- **For it:** it is a genuine named French fort, at the mountain, on the northern approaches
  — the same side as the September 1965 village operation. Distance is no objection to
  Larry's *extraction*: he was picked up by helicopter within about an hour, and 11 km is
  nothing by air.
- **Against it:** Larry's account has **Sgt. Lowry come running** from the fort to him about
  thirty minutes after the relay. Eleven kilometres does not fit a man arriving on foot.
  Either the fort in his memory is a different, smaller work close to the camp, or the
  sequence involved a flight, or the distance in his memory has compressed.

Note also that this index is built from **U.S. unit reports of 1966 and later**, so a small
colonial ruin at the mountain's base, used by a half A-team in 1964–65 and never occupied by
an American artillery battery, could be absent from it entirely. French works around the
mountain were not unique in any case — the Special Forces camp at Đồng Xoài was likewise
built on "the site of an old French fort" (`../units/callsigns-and-units.md`).

The useful next step is unchanged and now sharper: the **Pictomap sheet**, which should show
fortification symbols directly, at the base as well as to the north.

## Other named features worth keeping

- **The Saddle** (XT273602) between Núi Bà Đen and Núi Cau, and **The Rock Slide**
  (XT279603) on its north-east side — the terrain vocabulary Americans used for the massif.
- **Núi Bà Đen rock crusher / quarry** — a cluster of positions on the south-west side
  (XT265565 and neighbours). Worth noting because the USOM provincial report for November
  1965 records that Tây Ninh could not spend its road budget "if crushed rock from Nui Ba Den
  cannot be secured" (`../records-request/vnca/usom-provincial-reports-1965.md`). The same
  quarry, five years of war apart.
- **Trai Bi** (XT117708) north-west of the mountain, and **LZs Jupiter, Mars and Pluto**,
  CIDG landing zones north of it (1967) — the northern approaches, which is where the
  September 1965 village operation took place.

## 2026-09-01, later the same day — the 1:50,000 sheet itself, and A-324 confirmed twice

### The map is free and we have it

Sheet **6231-4** is online after all, at
`https://vva.vietnam.ttu.edu/images.php?img=/maps/PDF/6231-4.pdf` (7.8 MB PDF; a large JPEG
sits at `/maps/Big/6231-4.jpg`). `data/sources/map-l7014-6231-4-tay-ninh`. It is the right
sheet: **TÂY NINH, Sheet 6231 IV, Series L7014, 1:50,000**, with the massif in the
north-east quadrant and Tây Ninh city at centre-south.

**Read the credits before reading the map.** "PREPARED BY 29TH ENGR BN U S ARMY **1970** —
COMPILATION METHOD: PHOTO REVISION OF L7014 MAPS — PRINTED BY 29TH ENGR BN (BT) **11-70**."

This is a **1970 photo revision**, five years after Larry. It matters immediately, because
the sheet marks three settlements at and near the mountain **"(Destroyed)"** —
**Ấp Khe Dol** north-east of the massif, **Xóm Bãi Trại** at its southern foot, and
**Xã Tân Hưng** out to the north. The temptation is obvious: the September 1965 USOM report
describes an operation "north of the mountain… to burn down a VC village." **Resist it.**
A 1970 revision records the state of the ground in 1970, after Junction City, Attleboro,
Yellowstone and three more years of war. These annotations are not evidence about 1965 and
must not be cited as such. **What is needed is an earlier edition of the same sheet** — a
1965-or-before printing — and whether VNCA holds one is now a question worth asking.

### Place names around the mountain, from the sheet

The massif: **Núi Bà Đen** (986 m, with **Điện Bà** pagoda marked on the south face),
**Núi Cậu** to the north, **Núi Đất (Núi Heo)** to the west. Around the foot: **Ấp Khe Dol**
(NE, on Route 243), **Xóm Bãi Trại** (S), **Ấp Phước Hòa**, **Phú Khương** and **Ấp Phước
Long** (E, toward the city). North of the mountain: **Xã Tân Hưng**, and a scatter of ponds
named Bàu — Bàu Tam Ưng, Bàu Cỏ, Bàu Rò, Bàu Đe, Bàu Bom.

Any of these are candidates for the unnamed village in the September 1965 report; none is
identified. The 1:25,000 Pictomap halves (requested, issue #67) should carry more hamlet
names than this sheet does.

### The fort is on the map

At the road junction some ten kilometres north of the summit, near Bàu Tam Ưng, the sheet
carries an unmistakable **bastioned fortification symbol** with buildings inside it — the
"Old French Fort north of Nui Ba Den" of the position index, drawn. It is **the only fort
symbol near the mountain on this sheet**. At the south-west foot there is a walled compound
of buildings at the end of a road, which is the camp and quarry area, but no fort symbol.

That sharpens the candidacy without settling it. The northern fort is real and mapped; it is
still eleven kilometres from the summit, which still does not fit Sgt. Lowry arriving on
foot half an hour after the relay. And a 1970 sheet need not depict a small ruin that a half
A-team used in 1964–65.

### Detachment A-324, confirmed independently twice

Searching the archive on the designation rather than the place name — as intended — produced
two more documents, and both confirm it:

- **`data/sources/sf-camps-list-dickey`** (item 30560102001): "**A-324 Nui Ba Den, Tay Ninh,
  Jul64 – Oct 70 closed.**" The same table gives "**B-32 Tay Ninh, Mar65 – Nov 70**" and
  "B-320 Tay Ninh (TDY), Mar 65, converted to PCS," plus the province's other camps —
  A-322 Suối Đá (Apr65), Bến Sỏi, Prek Klok, Trảng Sụp, and A-325 Bàu Đồn (1966).
- **`data/sources/sf-camps-list-andrade`** (item 24990306001): tables Det **A-324** at
  **NUI BA DEN** with its grid, among the same B-32 camps.

**A discrepancy to keep, not to resolve.** This archive has recorded, from Kelly's official
history, that Detachment B-32 ran Tây Ninh province **from January 1965**. Both of these
lists say **March 1965**. Two months, two secondary sources against one; nothing here is
strong enough to overturn Kelly, and the difference is noted rather than decided.

### A model of the record we actually want

**`data/sources/5th-sfg-orll-1967`** (item 168300010050) is the 5th Special Forces Group's
Operational Report / Lessons Learned for the period ending 31 December 1967. Two years too
late — but its annexes are exactly the shape of document that would answer several open
questions at once. They tabulate **every detachment with its commander, camp, province, grid
coordinate, strike-force ethnicity, and a per-camp code word** (the III Corps camps carry
V-words — Venom for Tây Ninh, Virgo for Suối Đá, Vital for Trảng Sụp, and one against Núi Bà
Đen at XT281581). Another annex narrates patrols mounted from the mountain.

Two consequences:

1. **The 1965 equivalent of this report is a named document to request from NARA**, and it
   would carry Núi Bà Đen's grid, its detachment commander, and its code word for that year.
   If the boxing-term scheme Larry describes was in the 1965 annex, this is where it lives.
2. The 1967 code words are **not** boxing terms, which is consistent with schemes being
   reassigned over time and does nothing to weaken his account.

**A name coincidence, recorded so nobody trips on it later.** The 1967 detachment roster
shows an **A-325A commanded by a LT Lewis at Nui Ba Den**. Larry's **Major Lewis** flew
Rosethorn 007 in 1965 (issue #63, VN-0016). Different rank, different service branch role,
two years later, and no reason whatever to connect them. **They are not the same man on any
evidence we have**; the entry is noted only so a future session does not "discover" it and
mistake it for a lead.
